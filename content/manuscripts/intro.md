# Can we trust that the Bible we read is what they wrote?

None of the original pages written by Matthew, Mark, Luke, John or Paul survive. That is true of almost every book from the ancient world. What we have are copies, and copies of copies.

That sounds like bad news, but it is where the good news starts. The New Testament was copied more often than any other book from the ancient world. About 5,500 to 5,700 handwritten Greek copies survive, from small scraps to complete Bibles. Some fragments are probably from the 2nd century, and two whole Bibles survive from the 4th century.

With that many copies from so many places, scholars can compare them and see where a scribe slipped. When one copy goes wrong, the others show it. So the large number of differences between copies is a sign of how much we have to work with, not of a text that was lost.

On these pages we give you the real numbers, the oldest copies you can look at online, and an honest look at the few places where later copies added something. We also list some popular numbers we do not use, because the case is strong enough without them.
