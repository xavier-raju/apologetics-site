# What changed, and what didn't

## What changed
Scribes copied by hand, so they made mistakes. Most are tiny: a spelling, a word order (Greek can say "Jesus Christ" or "Christ Jesus" with the same meaning), a small word left out or repeated. Scholars estimate about 500,000 such differences, not counting spelling (Peter Gurry, 2016). The number is high because we have so many copies. A book with two copies has very few variants. A book with thousands has many.

A few changes are bigger. Three passages found in older Bibles like the King James are not in the earliest copies:

- **John 5:4**, the angel stirring the water at the pool of Bethesda. Probably added to explain why people waited by the pool (v. 7).
- **Mark 16:9-20**, the longer ending of Mark. The earliest complete copies, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, end at 16:8. The added ending sums up resurrection appearances also told in the other Gospels and Acts.
- **John 7:53-8:11**, the woman caught in adultery. Missing from the earliest copies and placed in different spots in later ones. Many scholars think it may preserve a true early story about Jesus, but it was not originally part of John.

Modern Bibles mark these with brackets or notes. Nobody is hiding them. We know about them precisely because we have so many early copies to check against.

## What didn't change
No core Christian belief rests on a disputed passage. Jesus' death and resurrection, his divinity, salvation by grace, and the Trinity are each taught in many passages whose wording is secure in the early copies. If you removed all three passages above, nothing in the creeds would change.

Even Bart Ehrman, the best-known skeptical scholar of the manuscripts, has said as much. In a Q&A printed in the 2005 paperback of Misquoting Jesus (HarperSanFrancisco, pp. 252-253), he says his view does not really conflict with Bruce Metzger's view that the essential Christian beliefs are not affected by the differences between manuscripts. To be fair to him, he adds that some differences do matter for how particular passages are read. (Paraphrased in our own words. Source seen: a transcription with photos of pp. 252-253; the book itself not yet checked, so we do not quote his exact words.)

In the Berean Standard Bible, Mark's Gospel in the earliest copies ends at 16:8: "So the women left the tomb and ran away, trembling and bewildered. And in their fear they did not say a word to anyone." (Mark 16:8, BSB)

## Answer card hook (for Alfred's card 4, "The Bible was changed")
- 30-second answer: We have thousands of early copies. Comparing them shows where scribes slipped, and modern Bibles footnote the few places where something was added. No core belief depends on those places.
