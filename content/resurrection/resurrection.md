# Did Jesus really rise?

Paul said that if Christ is not raised, our faith is empty (1 Corinthians 15:14). So Christianity invites testing right here.

## The minimal facts approach

Scholar Gary Habermas built a case using only facts that, he reports, the large majority of scholars accept, including many skeptics. Those percentages come from his own survey of the literature, so we share them as his claim, not as settled fact. Read his free article: https://www.garyhabermas.com/articles/Habermas_Minimal%20Facts%20STR%202012.pdf

The core facts:

1. **Jesus died by crucifixion.** Tacitus and Josephus confirm it (the Josephus mention sits in a passage that later Christian copyists partly added to), as do all Christian sources.
2. **His disciples sincerely believed they saw him alive again.** They said so from the start, and suffered for it.
3. **Paul, a persecutor, was changed.** He tells us himself (1 Corinthians 15:9; Galatians 1:13-16).
4. **James, Jesus' skeptical brother, was changed.** He did not believe during Jesus' ministry (John 7:5), then led the Jerusalem church.
5. **The tomb was empty.** Widely accepted, though less universally than the first four. Even Jesus' opponents claimed the body was stolen (Matthew 28:11-15), which assumes it was gone.

## The earliest report

"For what I received I passed on to you as of first importance: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that He was buried, that He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, and that He appeared to Cephas and then to the Twelve." (1 Corinthians 15:3-5, BSB)

Paul wrote this in the 50s, but he says he passed on what he had received. Many scholars, skeptics included, think Paul received it in the 30s, likely when he met Peter and James in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:18-19). See Habermas 2012, p. 25.

## Weighing the alternatives

| Theory | What it explains | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Disciples stole the body | Empty tomb | Why they suffered for a known lie; Paul and James |
| Jesus fainted (swoon) | Appearances | Roman execution; a wounded man doesn't inspire belief in victory over death |
| Wrong tomb | Empty tomb | Opponents could show the right one; the appearances |
| Hallucinations | Appearances | Groups; the empty tomb; Paul wasn't grieving |
| Legend grew over time | Later details | The creed is very early |
| He rose | All the facts | Requires openness to God acting |

We think the resurrection explains these facts best. Part of the question is whether we allow that God might act.

## Honest notes

- Not all twelve apostles are well attested as martyrs. Some are (Peter, James son of Zebedee, James the brother of Jesus). Sean McDowell grades each one: https://www.equip.org/articles/apostles-really-die-martyrs-faith/
- The Gospels differ on some details of Easter morning. They agree on the core.
- History can show the facts; trusting the risen Jesus is a step of faith built on them.
