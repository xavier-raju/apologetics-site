Answer an objection

No one wrote about Jesus during his lifetime.

30-second answer

That is true, and it is normal. Very few people in the ancient world, even important ones, were written about while alive; a traveling Jewish teacher would not be. What matters is how soon after: Paul writes within about 20 to 25 years, and passes on a creed that is earlier still.

Evidence

  • Paul's letters date to the 50s, roughly two decades after the crucifixion.
  • The creed in 1 Corinthians 15 is earlier still: many scholars, skeptics included, think Paul received it in the 30s (Galatians 1:18-19; Habermas 2012, p. 25).
  • Josephus and Tacitus wrote within about 60 to 85 years, which is good by ancient standards.
  • Arrian's biography of Alexander the Great, our main source, was written about 450 years after he died in 323 BC, drawing on earlier works now lost. Sources: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/arrian/ and https://www.livius.org/articles/person/alexander-the-great/

Still debated

How early the 1 Corinthians 15 creed is, and exactly when each Gospel was written, are still discussed.

Where this pointsThe earliest witnesses we have point to one event: Christ died, was buried, was raised and appeared to Cephas, the Twelve, James and more than five hundred others (1 Corinthians 15:3-8). See the evidence for the resurrection →
