  • Framingearly non-canonical· c. 170

    Diatessaron (Tatian)

    Diatessaron (Tatian) is a gospel outside the New Testament, and the repo dates it to c. 170.

    Go deeper

    The repo registry lists its standing today as: Canonical (historical/local): Syriac churches until the 5th c. Not canonical anywhere today. On ancient reception it notes: The standard gospel text of Syriac-speaking churches into the 5th c. Theodoret of Cyrrhus removed some 200 copies from his diocese, and Rabbula of Edessa ordered the separate gospels to be used. Surviving witnesses: Original (Syriac or Greek) lost. Witnesses include Ephrem's Syriac commentary (4th c.), the Arabic harmony, Codex Fuldensis (Latin, 546), and possibly the Dura-Europos fragment (before 256). Dates are scholarly estimates, not fixed facts.

    Today: Canonical (historical/local): Syriac churches until the 5th c. Not canonical anywhere today.. Ancient verdict: The standard gospel text of Syriac-speaking churches into the 5th c. Theodoret of Cyrrhus removed some 200 copies from his diocese, and Rabbula of Edessa ordered the separate gospels to be used.
