Prophecies

The repo's 100 prophecy entries. Framing: the New Testament itself makes the link. Contested: a later Christian reading or a possible allusion.

  • Contested1· Later Christian reading, with NT thematic parallels

    The woman's offspring defeats the serpent

    Genesis 3:15 → Romans 16:20, Galatians 4:4, Revelation 12:1-17

    Genesis 3:15 says the woman's offspring will strike the serpent's head. Later Christian readers took this as the first hint of Christ.

    Go deeper

    No New Testament writer quotes this verse as a prophecy. Paul says God will crush Satan under believers' feet (Romans 16:20), and Revelation 12 pictures a woman, her child and a dragon. The messianic reading is later Christian interpretation.

    • Genesis 3, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing2· Explicit NT application

    Blessing reaches the nations through Abraham's offspring

    Genesis 12:1-3, Genesis 18:18, Genesis 22:18 → Galatians 3:8, 16, 29, Acts 3:25-26

    God promises Abraham that all nations will be blessed through him. Paul and Acts apply this promise to Christ and the gospel.

    Go deeper

    Galatians 3 says scripture foresaw the Gentiles being justified, and reads 'offspring' as pointing to Christ. Acts 3 has Peter quote the promise. In Genesis the promise concerns Abraham's descendants in general.

    • Galatians 3, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested3· Promise plus Christian genealogical application

    The chosen line runs through Isaac

    Genesis 17:19, Genesis 21:12 → Matthew 1:2, Luke 3:34, Romans 9:7

    Genesis says the promise passes through Isaac. Matthew and Luke include Isaac in Jesus' family line.

    Go deeper

    The genealogies list Isaac as an ancestor, and Paul quotes 'through Isaac your offspring will be named' in Romans 9. Genesis presents this as a promise about Abraham's family, not about the Messiah, so the link is an interpretation.

  • Contested4· Promise and later messianic application

    The line continues through Jacob

    Genesis 28:13-14, Numbers 24:17 → Matthew 1:2, Luke 3:34

    The promise continues through Jacob. Matthew and Luke list Jacob among Jesus' ancestors.

    Go deeper

    Genesis 28 repeats the promise to Jacob, and Numbers 24 speaks of a star from Jacob. The Gospels use the genealogies rather than quoting these verses, so the link is an interpretation.

  • Framing5· Explicit NT identity claim; traditional messianic reading

    A ruler comes from Judah

    Genesis 49:8-12 → Matthew 1:2-3, Hebrews 7:14, Revelation 5:5

    Jacob's blessing says the sceptre will not depart from Judah. The New Testament says Jesus came from Judah.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 7:14 says our Lord was descended from Judah, and Revelation 5:5 calls him the Lion of the tribe of Judah. Jewish and Christian readers have long read Genesis 49 as messianic.

  • Framing6· Explicit NT application

    David's dynasty endures

    2 Samuel 7:12-16, Psalm 89:3-4, 19-37, Psalm 132:11-12 → Luke 1:32-33, Romans 1:3, Acts 2:30

    God promises David that his line and throne will last for ever. Luke, Paul and Acts apply this to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Luke 1 has the angel say Jesus will be given David's throne. Romans 1 says he was descended from David, and Acts 2 has Peter connect the oath to David with the resurrection.

  • Framing7· Explicit NT application

    A shoot grows from Jesse's line

    Isaiah 11:1-10 → Romans 15:12, Revelation 22:16

    Isaiah 11 speaks of a shoot from Jesse, David's father, who will rule with justice. Paul and Revelation apply it to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Romans 15:12 quotes the root of Jesse who will rule the Gentiles. Revelation 22:16 has Jesus call himself the root and descendant of David.

    • Isaiah 11, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested8· Later Christian reading with NT thematic parallels

    The righteous Branch restores just rule

    Jeremiah 23:5-6, Jeremiah 33:14-18 → Luke 1:32-33, 1 Corinthians 1:30

    Jeremiah promises a righteous Branch from David who will rule justly. Christian readers apply this to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    No New Testament passage quotes these verses directly. Luke 1 speaks of David's throne, and Paul calls Christ our righteousness in 1 Corinthians 1:30, which some read as an echo. The link is a later reading.

    • Passage
  • Framing9· Explicit NT application

    The king is God's son

    Psalm 2:7-9, 2 Samuel 7:14, 2 Samuel 5:5 → Acts 13:33, Hebrews 1:5

    Psalm 2 says to the king, you are my son. Acts and Hebrews apply this to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Acts 13:33 links the verse to the resurrection. Hebrews 1:5 quotes it together with 2 Samuel 7:14, I will be his father. In the psalm it speaks of Israel's anointed king.

    • Hebrews 1, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing10· Explicit NT application

    The anointed king has an enduring throne

    Psalm 45:6-7 → Hebrews 1:8-9

    Psalm 45 addresses the king, your throne, O God, endures for ever. Hebrews applies it to the Son.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 1:8-9 quotes the psalm to argue that the Son is above the angels. The psalm was written for a royal wedding.

    • Hebrews 1, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested11· Explicit NT application

    The virgin or young woman and Immanuel

    Isaiah 7:14-16 → Matthew 1:22-23

    Isaiah 7:14 speaks of a young woman, or virgin, bearing a son called Immanuel. Matthew says Jesus' birth fulfilled it.

    Go deeper

    Matthew quotes the Greek translation, which uses the word for virgin. The Hebrew word usually means young woman. In Isaiah the sign is given to King Ahaz during a war, so readers disagree on how the verse applies. Marked Contested because the Hebrew word and the verse's original setting are disputed.

    Bethlehem as the ruler's origin

    Micah 5:2-5 → Matthew 2:5-6, John 7:41-42

    Micah says a ruler will come from Bethlehem. Matthew quotes it about Jesus' birth.

    Go deeper

    In Matthew the chief priests and scribes cite Micah to Herod. John 7 reports people saying the Messiah must come from Bethlehem.

    • Micah 5, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition

    A royal child brings justice and peace

    Isaiah 9:6-7 → Luke 1:32-33, Luke 2:11-14

    Isaiah 9 speaks of a child born to rule with justice and peace on David's throne. Christians read it as about Jesus.

    Go deeper

    The New Testament does not quote these verses directly, though Luke 1 echoes the language of David's throne without end. The messianic reading is traditional.

  • Contested14· NT allusion and later interpretation

    A star rises from Jacob

    Numbers 24:17 → Matthew 2:1-12, Revelation 22:16

    Numbers 24:17 speaks of a star coming out of Jacob. Christian readers link it to the star in Matthew.

    Go deeper

    Matthew's magi follow a star but Matthew does not quote Numbers. Revelation 22:16 calls Jesus the bright morning star. The link is an allusion and later interpretation.

    Foreigners bring tribute

    Psalm 72:10-11, Isaiah 60:1-6 → Matthew 2:1-12

    Psalm 72 and Isaiah 60 speak of kings bringing gifts. Christians link them to the magi's gifts in Matthew.

    Go deeper

    Matthew does not quote these passages. Isaiah 60 mentions gold and frankincense. The link is a later Christian reading.

  • Framing16· Explicit NT typology

    God's son comes out of Egypt

    Hosea 11:1 → Matthew 2:14-15

    Hosea says, out of Egypt I called my son. Matthew applies it to the family's return from Egypt.

    Go deeper

    In Hosea the verse looks back to Israel's exodus. Matthew reads Jesus' story as repeating Israel's, which is a pattern reading rather than a prediction.

    • Hosea 11, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing17· Explicit NT application

    Rachel weeps for her children

    Jeremiah 31:15-17 → Matthew 2:16-18

    Jeremiah describes Rachel weeping for her children. Matthew applies it to the killing of the boys at Bethlehem.

    Go deeper

    In Jeremiah the passage concerns Israel's exile and goes on to promise return. Matthew uses it as a fulfilment of the mourning.

  • Framing18· Explicit NT fulfillment claim with an unidentified source

    He will be called a Nazorean

    proposed connections include Isaiah 11:1 and Judges 13:5 → Matthew 2:23

    Matthew says Jesus would be called a Nazorean, as the prophets said. No such verse exists in the Old Testament.

    Go deeper

    Scholars suggest links to the 'branch' (netzer) of Isaiah 11:1 or the Nazirite of Judges 13:5. Matthew's source for the words is not identified.

    • Infancy Narrative Commentaries Boston College, Clough School of Theology and Ministry
  • Contested19· Narrative typology

    The endangered child repeats Moses's story

    Exodus 1:15-2:10, Exodus 4:19 → Matthew 2:13-21

    Matthew's story of the infant Jesus escaping a king's killing echoes Moses' birth story in Exodus.

    Go deeper

    Matthew does not cite Exodus here, but the angel's words, those seeking the child's life are dead, closely match Exodus 4:19. The parallel is a narrative pattern.

  • Framing20· Explicit NT application to John the Baptist

    Elijah returns before the decisive day

    Malachi 4:5-6, or 3:23-24 in some editions → Luke 1:17, Matthew 11:14, John 1:21, Matthew 17:10-13

    Malachi says God will send Elijah before the day of the Lord. The Gospels apply this to John the Baptist.

    Go deeper

    Luke 1:17 says John will go in the spirit and power of Elijah. Matthew 11 and 17 have Jesus identify John with Elijah. In John 1:21 the Baptist himself says he is not Elijah.

    • Malachi 3, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition

    Isaiah 40 speaks of a voice in the wilderness preparing the Lord's way. All four Gospels apply it to John the Baptist.

    Go deeper

    Mark, Matthew and Luke quote it about John. In John's Gospel the Baptist uses the words about himself.

    Malachi 3:1, Exodus 23:20 → Mark 1:2, Matthew 11:10, Luke 7:27

    Malachi 3:1 speaks of a messenger sent ahead. The Gospels apply it to John the Baptist.

    Go deeper

    Mark combines it with Isaiah 40 and attributes both to Isaiah. Matthew and Luke have Jesus quote it about John.

  • Contested23· NT allusion

    The chosen son receives the Spirit

    Psalm 2:7, Isaiah 42:1 → Mark 1:9-11 and parallels

    At Jesus' baptism the heavenly voice echoes Psalm 2 and Isaiah 42.

    Go deeper

    Mark's 'you are my beloved son' recalls Psalm 2:7, and 'with you I am well pleased' recalls Isaiah 42:1. The Gospels do not cite these passages here, so the link is an allusion.

  • Framing24· Explicit quotation within typology

    Faithful Israel succeeds in the wilderness

    Deuteronomy 6:13, 16, Deuteronomy 8:2-3 → Matthew 4:1-11, Luke 4:1-13

    When tempted, Jesus answers the devil three times with verses from Deuteronomy.

    Go deeper

    Matthew and Luke both report the quotations. Deuteronomy recalls Israel's forty years in the wilderness, and the Gospels' forty days are often read as a parallel.

    Isaiah speaks of light dawning on Galilee. Matthew applies it to Jesus settling in Capernaum.

    Go deeper

    An anointed herald announces good news and release

    Isaiah 61:1-2, Isaiah 58:6 → Luke 4:16-21

    In Luke, Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in the Nazareth synagogue and says it is fulfilled today.

    Go deeper

    Luke's reading combines Isaiah 61:1-2 with a line from Isaiah 58:6. The fulfilment claim is made by Jesus in Luke's account.

  • Contested27· NT allusive answer to a messianic question

    The blind see, the lame walk, and the poor receive news

    Isaiah 35:5-6, Isaiah 61:1, Isaiah 26:19 → Matthew 11:2-6, Luke 7:18-23

    When John asks if Jesus is the one to come, Jesus answers with phrases from Isaiah about the blind, lame and deaf.

    Go deeper

    Matthew and Luke report the answer. The phrases echo Isaiah 35 and 61 but are not presented as a single quotation.

    The servant bears illnesses

    Isaiah 53:4 → Matthew 8:16-17

    Matthew says Jesus' healings fulfilled Isaiah 53:4, he took our illnesses.

    Go deeper

    Matthew applies the verse to physical healing. Other New Testament writers, such as 1 Peter, apply Isaiah 53 to Jesus' suffering instead.

    The servant acts gently and brings justice

    Isaiah 42:1-4 → Matthew 12:15-21

    Matthew quotes Isaiah 42, God's gentle servant who brings justice, about Jesus.

    Go deeper

    This is the longest Old Testament quotation in Matthew. He applies it to Jesus telling people not to make him known.

  • Framing30· Explicit NT application and echo

    A light for the Gentiles

    Isaiah 42:6, Isaiah 49:6 → Luke 2:29-32, Acts 13:47

    Isaiah speaks of a light for the Gentiles. Simeon in Luke and Paul in Acts apply it.

    Go deeper

    Simeon's song in Luke 2 echoes Isaiah. In Acts 13:47, Paul and Barnabas apply the verse to their own mission to Gentiles.

  • Framing31· Explicit NT application

    A prophet like Moses

    Deuteronomy 18:15-19 → John 6:14, Acts 3:22-23, Acts 7:37

    Moses says God will raise up a prophet like him. Acts and John apply this to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Peter quotes Deuteronomy 18 in Acts 3, and Stephen does in Acts 7. In John 6 the crowd calls Jesus the prophet who is to come.

  • Framing32· Explicit NT application

    Teaching through parables

    Psalm 78:2 → Matthew 13:34-35

    Matthew says Jesus' teaching in parables fulfilled Psalm 78:2.

    Go deeper

    Matthew attributes the words to 'the prophet'. Psalm 78 is a psalm of Asaph that retells Israel's history.

    • Matthew 13, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing33· Explicit NT application

    Hearers fail to understand

    Isaiah 6:9-10 → Mark 4:10-12, Matthew 13:14-15, John 12:39-41, Acts 28:25-27

    Isaiah 6 says people will hear but not understand. Several New Testament writers apply it to those who rejected Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Mark, Matthew, John and Acts all use the passage. Matthew quotes it in full, and Acts has Paul quote it at the end of the book.

    • Matthew 13, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing34· Explicit NT application

    Lips honor God while hearts remain distant

    Isaiah 29:13 → Mark 7:6-7, Matthew 15:7-9

    Isaiah says this people honours God with their lips while their hearts are far away. Jesus quotes it in Mark and Matthew.

    Go deeper

    Jesus applies it to a dispute over washing and tradition. Mark and Matthew follow the Greek translation.

  • Framing35· Explicit NT application

    The servant's message encounters disbelief

    Isaiah 53:1 → John 12:37-38, Romans 10:16

    Isaiah 53:1 asks, who has believed our message? John and Paul apply it to unbelief.

    Go deeper

    John uses it to explain why many did not believe despite the signs. Paul uses it in Romans 10 about Israel and the gospel.

  • Framing36· Explicit NT application

    Hatred without cause

    Psalm 35:19, Psalm 69:4 → John 15:24-25

    The psalms speak of being hated without cause. In John, Jesus applies this to himself.

    Go deeper

    John 15:25 says this fulfils what is written in 'their law'. The words match Psalm 35:19 and Psalm 69:4.

  • Contested37· Later Christian reading

    Psalm 69 speaks of becoming a stranger to one's own family. Christian readers link it to Jesus' family not believing in him.

    Ezekiel 34 and Psalm 23 picture God as a shepherd gathering the flock. John has Jesus call himself the good shepherd.

    John does not quote these passages, but the imagery is close. Matthew says Jesus saw the crowds as sheep without a shepherd.

    Israel received manna in the wilderness. In John, Jesus calls himself the true bread from heaven.

    Go deeper

    The crowd quotes 'he gave them bread from heaven to eat', and Jesus contrasts the manna with himself. John presents it as a pattern, not a prediction.

    Go deeper

  • Contested43· Typology and later prophetic linkage

    Jesus as the temple

    2 Samuel 7:13, Zechariah 6:12-13 → Revelation 21:22, John 2:19-22

    Christians read Jesus as the new temple. John says he spoke of his body as the temple.

    Go deeper

    John 2 says Jesus meant his body when he said 'destroy this temple'. Revelation 21 says the Lamb is the city's temple. Linking this to 2 Samuel 7 and Zechariah 6 is a later reading.

  • Contested44· Scriptural allusion with an unidentified exact quotation

    Living water flows outward

    Isaiah 44:3, Ezekiel 47:1-12, Zechariah 14:8 → John 7:37-39

    In John, Jesus says rivers of living water will flow from within, 'as the scripture has said'. No exact verse matches.

    Go deeper

    John explains it as the Spirit. Isaiah 44, Ezekiel 47 and Zechariah 14 speak of water flowing out, and are proposed as the source.

    • John 7, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing45· Explicit comparison and narrative typology

    1 Kings 17:8-24, 2 Kings 4:18-44, 2 Kings 7:11-17 → Luke 4:25-27, John 6:1-14

    Jesus compares himself to Elijah and Elisha, and some Gospel miracles echo theirs.

    Go deeper

    In Luke 4 Jesus cites the widow of Zarephath and Naaman. The feeding in John 6 resembles Elisha feeding a hundred people with barley loaves (2 Kings 4).

  • Contested59· Explicit fulfillment claim with composite or problematic attribution

  • Framing64· Explicit NT application

    Rulers gather against the anointed one

    Psalm 2:1-2 → Acts 4:25-28

    Psalm 2 speaks of rulers gathering against the Lord's anointed. In Acts the believers apply it to Herod and Pilate.

    Go deeper

    Acts 4:25-28 quotes the psalm in a prayer and names Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel.

    • Passage
  • Contested65· Probable NT allusion and Christian typology

    The righteous son is tested by a shameful death

    Wisdom of Solomon 2:12-20 → Matthew 27:43

    Wisdom 2 has the wicked plan to test a righteous man who calls himself God's son. Matthew's mockery scene is similar.

    Go deeper

    Matthew 27:43 reports mockers saying, he trusts in God, let God deliver him, for he said, I am God's son. The wording echoes Psalm 22:8 and Wisdom 2:18, but Matthew does not cite Wisdom.

    • Wisdom 2, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested66· NT allusion

    Mockery and challenges to divine rescue

    Psalm 22:7-8 → Mark 15:29-32, Matthew 27:39-43

    Psalm 22 describes mockers who wag their heads and say, let the Lord rescue him. Mark and Matthew describe similar mockery at the cross.

    Go deeper

    Neither Gospel cites the psalm here, but the head-wagging and the taunt echo it closely.

    • Mark 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing67· Explicit quotation attributed to Jesus

    The cry of abandonment

    Psalm 22:1 → Mark 15:34, Matthew 27:46

    Mark and Matthew report that Jesus cries out from the cross with the opening line of Psalm 22: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?

    Go deeper

    The Gospels present Jesus speaking the psalm's words himself rather than calling them a prediction. Psalm 22 is a prayer of a sufferer who ends by praising God for rescue. Readers disagree whether quoting the first line points to the whole psalm, so the link is shown as an interpretation.

    • Psalm 22, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested68· Later explicit Christian interpretation; not a direct NT quotation of this verse

    Hands and feet pierced

    Psalm 22:16, Hebrew verse 17 → Luke 24:39-40, John 20:25-27

    Christians have long read Psalm 22:16 (pierced hands and feet) as pointing to the crucifixion, though the New Testament never quotes this verse.

    Go deeper

    The Hebrew wording of the line is disputed: many manuscripts read like a lion, while the Greek translation and some Hebrew evidence support a verb like dig or pierce. John 20 mentions the nail marks but does not cite the psalm. This is a later Christian reading and is marked contested.

    • Piercing the Ambiguities of Psalm 22:16 and the Messiah's Mission Conrad R. Gren, Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society 48/2 (2005), 283-299, 2005
  • Framing69· Explicit NT application in John

    Clothes divided and lots cast

    Psalm 22:18 → Mark 15:24, John 19:23-24

    John says the soldiers divided Jesus's clothes and cast lots for his tunic to fulfil Psalm 22:18.

    Go deeper

    Mark also reports the dividing of clothes using the psalm's wording, without calling it a fulfilment. John makes the link explicit. The link is the Gospel writer's own reading of the psalm.

    • John 19, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested70· Possible NT allusion

    Extreme thirst

    Psalm 22:15 → John 19:28

    Psalm 22 speaks of a tongue stuck to the jaws. John says Jesus said, I thirst, to fulfil scripture.

    Go deeper

    John does not name which scripture. Psalm 22:15 and Psalm 69:21 are both proposed.

    • Passage
  • Contested71· NT allusion and fulfillment context

    Gall or sour wine

    Psalm 69:21 → Matthew 27:34, 48, Mark 15:23, 36, John 19:28-30

    Psalm 69 says they gave me gall for food and vinegar for my thirst. The Gospels report Jesus being offered wine and sour wine.

    Go deeper

    Matthew's wording, wine mixed with gall, is close to the psalm. Mark says wine mixed with myrrh. John says Jesus received the sour wine.

    • Passage
  • Framing72· Explicit quotation

    Entrusting his spirit to God

    Psalm 31:5 → Luke 23:46

    Luke reports Jesus' last words as, into your hands I commit my spirit, from Psalm 31.

    Go deeper

    Only Luke has these words. Mark and Matthew report a loud cry, and John reports 'it is finished'.

    • Passage
  • Framing73· Explicit NT fulfillment claim

    No bone is broken

    Exodus 12:46, Numbers 9:12, Psalm 34:20 → John 19:33-36

    John says the soldiers did not break Jesus's legs so that the Scripture would be fulfilled: not one of his bones will be broken.

    Go deeper

    The words echo the rule for the Passover lamb in Exodus 12 and Numbers 9, and a line about the righteous in Psalm 34. John uses this to present Jesus as the Passover lamb. The link is John's explicit claim, shown as framing.

    • John 19, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing74· Explicit NT application

    The pierced figure is seen and mourned

    Zechariah 12:10 → John 19:34-37, Revelation 1:7

    John links the spear thrust to Zechariah 12:10, which speaks of looking on the one they pierced.

    Go deeper

    In Zechariah the verse is part of an oracle about Jerusalem mourning. John applies it to the crucifixion, and Revelation 1:7 applies it to Jesus's return. Both uses are New Testament readings of the prophet.

    • Zechariah 12, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested75· Possible NT allusion

    Darkness at noon

    Amos 8:9-10 → Mark 15:33, Matthew 27:45, Luke 23:44-45

    Amos speaks of the sun going down at noon. Mark, Matthew and Luke report darkness from the sixth to the ninth hour.

    Go deeper

    None of the Gospels quotes Amos here. The link is an allusion some readers see.

    • Passage
  • Framing76· Explicit NT application

    Counted among transgressors

    Isaiah 53:12 → Luke 22:37, Luke 23:32-33

    Isaiah 53 says the servant was counted among transgressors. In Luke, Jesus quotes it about himself.

    Go deeper

    Luke 22:37 has Jesus quote it at the last meal. Some manuscripts of Mark 15 add it at the crucifixion of the two robbers, but most modern editions leave it out.

    • Mark 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested77· NT allusion and later explicit Christian interpretation

    Burial associated with a rich man

    Isaiah 53:9 → Matthew 27:57-60

    Isaiah 53:9 says the servant's grave was with the wicked and with a rich man, and Christians connect this with Joseph of Arimathea's tomb.

    Go deeper

    Matthew calls Joseph a rich man but does not quote Isaiah here. The Hebrew line is hard to translate and can be read in more than one way. The link is a later Christian reading and is marked contested.

    • Isaiah 53, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing78· Explicit NT application and allusion

    The servant suffers for others

    Isaiah 52:13-53:12 → Mark 10:45, Acts 8:30-35, 1 Peter 2:22-25, Hebrews 9:28

    Several New Testament writers read Isaiah's servant who suffers for others (Isaiah 52:13 to 53:12) as describing Jesus.

    Go deeper

    Acts 8 has Philip explain the passage as being about Jesus, and 1 Peter 2 uses its language about wounds that heal. In Isaiah the servant is not named, and Jewish readers have often taken him to be Israel. The Christian reading is old and explicit, but it remains an interpretation.

    • 1 Peter 2, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing79· Explicit NT typology

    The Passover lamb

    Exodus 12 → John 1:29, 1 Corinthians 5:7, John 19:14, 36

    Paul calls Christ our Passover lamb, and John times Jesus's death to the Passover preparation.

    Go deeper

    Exodus 12 tells how the lamb's blood marked Israel's houses on the night of the exodus. The New Testament uses this as a pattern for Jesus's death rather than as a prediction. This kind of pattern reading is called typology.

    • Passage
  • Contested80· Typology, with NT verbal and conceptual parallels

    Isaac, the beloved son offered and restored

    Genesis 22:1-19 → Romans 8:32, Hebrews 11:17-19

    Abraham is told to offer his son Isaac, who is spared. Paul and Hebrews echo the story about God's Son.

    Go deeper

    Romans 8:32, he did not spare his own Son, recalls Genesis 22. Hebrews 11 says Abraham believed God could raise the dead. Reading Isaac as a type of Christ is an interpretation.

    • Passage
  • Framing81· Explicit NT and early Christian typology

    The Day of Atonement and sacrificial access

    Leviticus 16, later Epistle of Barnabas 7 → Hebrews 9:6-14, 24-28, Hebrews 10:1-18

    Leviticus 16 describes the high priest entering the holy place once a year. Hebrews applies the pattern to Christ.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 9 says Christ entered once for all with his own blood. The Epistle of Barnabas 7, written later, also reads the Day of Atonement this way.

    • Passage
  • Framing82· Explicit NT application

    The curse of hanging on a tree

    Deuteronomy 21:22-23 → Galatians 3:13, Acts 5:30

    Deuteronomy says a hanged body is under God's curse. Paul says Christ became a curse for us.

    Go deeper

    Galatians 3:13 quotes the verse. Acts 5:30 and 10:39 speak of Jesus being hanged on a tree.

    • Galatians 3, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested83· Later Christian chronological identification; partial explicit NT reuse of the chapter

    An anointed one is cut off

    Daniel 9:24-27 → Matthew 24:15, Mark 13:14

    Daniel 9 speaks of an anointed one cut off after a set number of weeks. Some Christians use it to calculate when the Messiah would come.

    Go deeper

    The Gospels refer to Daniel's 'desolating sacrilege' (Mark 13:14), but do not apply the 'cut off' verse to Jesus. The calculation of the weeks is debated.

    • Daniel 9, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing84· Explicit NT application

    A new covenant

    Jeremiah 31:31-34 → Luke 22:20, 1 Corinthians 11:25, Hebrews 8:8-12, Hebrews 10:16-17

    Jeremiah promises a new covenant written on the heart. The Gospels, Paul and Hebrews link it to Jesus.

    Go deeper

    At the last meal, Luke and Paul report Jesus speaking of the new covenant in his blood. Hebrews 8 quotes Jeremiah 31 in full.

    • Hebrews 8, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing85· Explicit NT typology and verbal echo

    Covenant ratified through blood

    Exodus 24:3-8 → Mark 14:24, Matthew 26:28, Hebrews 9:18-22

    Moses seals the covenant with blood, saying, see the blood of the covenant. Jesus' words at the last meal echo it.

    Go deeper

    Mark and Matthew report 'my blood of the covenant'. Hebrews 9 quotes Exodus 24 directly.

    • Passage
  • Framing86· Explicit NT application

    Obedience and a prepared body

    Psalm 40:6-8 → Hebrews 10:5-10

    Psalm 40 says God desired obedience, not sacrifice. Hebrews applies it to Christ.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 10 quotes the Greek version, 'a body you have prepared for me', which differs from the Hebrew 'you have opened my ears'.

    • Hebrews 10, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Contested87· Possible scriptural background, not an explicit NT quotation of Hosea

    Revival on the third day

    Hosea 6:1-3 → 1 Corinthians 15:4, Luke 24:46

    Hosea 6:2 speaks of God raising his people on the third day, and some readers see it behind the early claim that Jesus was raised on the third day.

    Go deeper

    Paul says Christ was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures but does not name which Scriptures. No New Testament text quotes Hosea for this. The connection is possible background only.

    • Hosea 6, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing88· Explicit NT typology

    Jonah's three days

    Jonah 1:17 → Luke 11:29-32, Matthew 12:39-40

    In Matthew, Jesus compares his coming three days in the earth to Jonah's three days inside the great fish.

    Go deeper

    Jesus is reported using Jonah as a sign to his own generation. Luke's version of the saying focuses on Jonah's preaching instead of the three days. Matthew presents it as a pattern, not as a prophecy about the Messiah in the book of Jonah.

    • Passage
  • Framing89· Explicit NT application

    The holy one does not undergo corruption

    Psalm 16:8-11, Psalm 13:35-37 → Acts 2:25-32

    In Acts, Peter and Paul argue that Psalm 16, which says God's holy one will not see decay, was about Jesus's resurrection.

    Go deeper

    Their argument in Acts 2 and Acts 13 is that David died and his tomb was known, so the psalm must point beyond him. In the psalm the speaker expresses trust that God will not abandon him to the grave. The application to Jesus is the apostles' reading.

    • Acts, Chapter 13 Orthodox Church in America
  • Contested90· Christian reading of the servant's vindication

    The suffering servant sees life and vindication

    Isaiah 53:10-12, the Christological reading of the servant in Acts 8:35 and 1 Peter 2:21-25 →

    Christians read the end of Isaiah 53, where the servant sees his offspring and is vindicated after suffering, as pointing to the resurrection.

    Go deeper

    The passage does not use the word resurrection, and its Hebrew is difficult in places. The New Testament applies Isaiah 53 to Jesus but does not quote these verses for the resurrection directly. The link is marked contested.

    • Isaiah 53, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing91· Explicit NT application

    David's enduring promises become effective

    Isaiah 55:3 → Acts 13:34

    Isaiah 55 speaks of the sure mercies promised to David. In Acts, Paul applies this to Jesus' resurrection.

    Go deeper

    Acts 13:34 quotes it in Paul's sermon at Pisidian Antioch, alongside Psalm 16.

    • Passage
  • Framing92· Explicit NT application to future resurrection

    Death is finally defeated

    Isaiah 25:8, Hosea 13:14 → 1 Corinthians 15:54-57

    Isaiah says God will swallow up death, and Hosea asks, where is your sting? Paul quotes both about the future resurrection.

    Go deeper

    1 Corinthians 15:54-55 combines the two passages. Paul applies them to the resurrection of believers at the end.

    • 1 Corinthians 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing93· Explicit NT application

    The vindicated one joins his brothers in praise

    Psalm 22:22 → Hebrews 2:11-12

    Psalm 22 ends with the sufferer praising God among his brothers. Hebrews puts these words in Christ's mouth.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 2:12 quotes Psalm 22:22. The psalm moves from suffering to praise.

    • Passage
  • Framing94· Explicit NT application

    Enthronement at God's right hand

    Psalm 110:1 → Mark 12:35-37, Acts 2:34-36, Hebrews 1:13, Mark 14:62

    Psalm 110 says, sit at my right hand. It is the Old Testament verse the New Testament quotes most.

    Go deeper

    In Mark 12 Jesus asks how David can call the Messiah Lord. Acts 2 applies it to the resurrection and ascension, and Hebrews 1 quotes it too.

    • Psalm 110, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing95· Explicit NT application and allusion

    The humanlike figure receives dominion

    Daniel 7:13-14, 18, 27 → Mark 14:62, Matthew 24:30, Revelation 1:7, 13

    Daniel 7 sees one like a son of man coming with the clouds and receiving dominion. Jesus uses this language in the Gospels.

    Go deeper

    At his trial in Mark 14:62, Jesus combines Daniel 7 with Psalm 110. Revelation 1 also draws on it.

    • Daniel 7, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing96· Explicit NT typology and application

    A permanent priest in Melchizedek's order

    Genesis 14:18-20, Psalm 110:4 → Hebrews 5:5-10, Hebrews 7:1-28

    Psalm 110 calls the king a priest for ever in the order of Melchizedek. Hebrews applies it to Christ.

    Go deeper

    Hebrews 5 and 7 build on Genesis 14, where Melchizedek blesses Abraham, and on Psalm 110:4.

    • Hebrews 7, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing97· Explicit NT application

    Ascension and gifts

    Psalm 68:18 → Ephesians 4:7-13

    Psalm 68 speaks of ascending on high and receiving gifts. Ephesians applies it to Christ giving gifts to the church.

    Go deeper

    Ephesians 4:8 has 'gave gifts' where the Hebrew psalm has 'received gifts'.

    • Ephesians 4, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing98· Explicit NT typology

    The renewed Adam and restored human vocation

    Genesis 1:26-28, Genesis 2:7, Psalm 8:4-6 → Romans 5:12-21, 1 Corinthians 15:21-28, 45-49, Hebrews 2:5-9

    Paul and Hebrews present Christ as a new Adam who fulfils humanity's role over creation.

    Go deeper

    Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15 contrast Adam and Christ. Hebrews 2 applies Psalm 8 on humanity's rule to Jesus.

    • 1 Corinthians 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • Framing99· Explicit NT application to Pentecost

    The Spirit is poured out

    Joel 2:28-32, or 3:1-5 → Acts 2:16-21

    Joel says God will pour out his Spirit on all people. In Acts, Peter says this happened at Pentecost.

    Go deeper

    Acts 2:16-21 quotes Joel at length. Peter adds 'in the last days' to Joel's wording.

    • Passage
  • Framing100· Explicit NT application to the post-Jesus movement

    David's fallen tent is restored and Gentiles are included

    Amos 9:11-12 → Acts 15:13-18

    Amos says God will rebuild David's fallen tent so the nations may seek him. In Acts, James applies it to Gentile believers.

    Go deeper

    Acts 15 quotes a form close to the Greek translation, which differs from the Hebrew wording about possessing Edom.

    • Acts 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
