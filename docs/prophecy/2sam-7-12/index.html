God's covenant with David: an eternal throne

pattern

Old Testament · 2 Samuel 7:12-16

12 And when your days are fulfilled and you rest with your fathers, I will raise up your descendant after you, who will come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom.

13 He will build a house for My Name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.

14 I will be his Father, and he will be My son. When he does wrong, I will discipline him with the rod of men and with the blows of the sons of men.

15 But My loving devotion will never be removed from him as I removed it from Saul, whom I removed from before you.

16 Your house and kingdom will endure forever before Me, and your throne will be established forever.”

New Testament

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David,

33 and He will reign over the house of Jacob forever. His kingdom will never end!”

5 For to which of the angels did God ever say: “You are My Son; today I have become Your Father”? Or again: “I will be His Father, and He will be My Son”?

22 After removing Saul, He raised up David as their king and testified about him: ‘I have found David son of Jesse a man after My own heart; he will carry out My will in its entirety.’

23 From the descendants of this man, God has brought to Israel the Savior Jesus, as He promised.

Two readings, fairly stated

God promises David a descendant whose throne will last forever, and says 'I will be his Father, and he will be My son.' Gabriel's words to Mary and the opening of Hebrews apply this promise to Jesus.

Jewish readers see this as God's covenant with David's dynasty, first fulfilled in Solomon, who built the Temple, and the basis of the hope for a future anointed king from David's line.

Note. Hebrews 1:5 quotes only the first half of 7:14. The second half, about punishing wrongdoing, fits Solomon and later kings, which is why this is a pattern rather than a one-to-one prediction.
