The virgin (or young woman) will bear a son: Immanuel

direct predictionContested

Old Testament · Isaiah 7:14

14 Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, the virgin will be with child and give birth to a son, and will call Him Immanuel.

New Testament

22 All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had said through the prophet:

23 “Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and they will call Him Immanuel” (which means, “God with us”).

The original words

Tap a word to see its meaning.

Two readings, fairly stated

Matthew sees the virgin birth as the full meaning of the sign given to the house of David: a child whose name means 'God with us.' Isaiah 7-11 goes on to describe a child who rules on David's throne (9:6-7; 11:1-10), so many Christians read the sign as reaching beyond Ahaz's own time. Conservative scholars also note that almah is never clearly used in the Hebrew Bible of a married woman.

Jewish interpreters read the verse as a sign for King Ahaz in his own day: a young woman already known to him would have a son, and before the boy was old enough to choose between good and bad, the two kings threatening Judah would be gone (7:16). The Hebrew word almah means a young woman, and the usual word for virgin is betulah. The child is often identified as a son of Ahaz (such as Hezekiah) or of the prophet.

Note. Matthew follows the Greek Septuagint, which had already translated almah as parthenos ('virgin') before Jesus was born. A 'near and far' (dual) fulfilment reading is common among Christian scholars.
