A child born, called Mighty God

direct predictionContested

Old Testament · Isaiah 9:6-7

6 For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given, and the government will be upon His shoulders. And He will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.

7 Of the increase of His government and peace there will be no end. He will reign on the throne of David and over his kingdom, to establish and sustain it with justice and righteousness from that time and forevermore. The zeal of the LORD of Hosts will accomplish this.

New Testament

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David,

33 and He will reign over the house of Jacob forever. His kingdom will never end!”

13 Leaving Nazareth, He went and lived in Capernaum, which is by the sea in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali,

14 to fulfill what was spoken through the prophet Isaiah:

15 “Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the Way of the Sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles—

16 the people living in darkness have seen a great light; on those living in the land of the shadow of death, a light has dawned.”

The original words

Tap a word to see its meaning.

Two readings, fairly stated

A child of David is given titles that sound divine, including 'Mighty God' (El Gibbor), the same title Isaiah uses for the LORD himself a chapter later (10:21). He rules on David's throne forever. The angel's words to Mary in Luke 1:32-33 echo this promise, and Matthew 4 ties the light dawning on Galilee (Isaiah 9:1-2) to Jesus' ministry.

Jewish readers generally see the passage as celebrating a king of Judah, most often Hezekiah. They read the long name as a throne name that praises God, along the lines of 'the Mighty God is planning wonders', rather than as titles describing the child's own nature. In Hebrew Bibles this verse is numbered 9:5.

Note. The New Testament never quotes 9:6 with a fulfilment formula. The link is drawn from its themes and from 9:1-2, which Matthew does quote.
