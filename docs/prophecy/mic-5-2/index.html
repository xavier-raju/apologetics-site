A ruler from Bethlehem

direct prediction

Old Testament · Micah 5:2

2 But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are small among the clans of Judah, out of you will come forth for Me One to be ruler over Israel—One whose origins are of old, from the days of eternity.

New Testament

4 And when he had assembled all the chief priests and scribes of the people, he asked them where the Christ was to be born.

5 “In Bethlehem in Judea,” they replied, “for this is what the prophet has written:

6 ‘But you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah, for out of you will come a ruler who will be the shepherd of My people Israel.’”

42 Doesn’t the Scripture say that the Christ will come from the line of David and from Bethlehem, the village where David lived?”

The original words

Tap a word to see its meaning.

Two readings, fairly stated

Micah names little Bethlehem, David's hometown, as the place a coming ruler of Israel would come from. In Matthew 2 the chief priests and scribes cite this verse when Herod asks where the Messiah is to be born, which shows it was already read messianically. Christians also note the ruler's origins are 'from of old, from the days of eternity.'

Jewish tradition also reads this as a messianic promise: the Messiah will come from David's line, which traces back to Bethlehem. The phrase about ancient origins is usually understood as the long-ago roots of David's family, not as a person existing before birth. In Hebrew Bibles the verse is Micah 5:1.

Note. This is one of the few passages that Jews and Christians both read messianically. The disagreement is about who fulfils it, not whether it points to the Messiah.
