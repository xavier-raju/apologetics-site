Resurrection and exaltation

The LORD said to my Lord, 'Sit at My right hand'

direct prediction

Old Testament · Psalm 110:1,4

1 A Psalm of David. The LORD said to my Lord: “Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet.”

4 The LORD has sworn and will not change His mind: “You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.”

New Testament

41 While the Pharisees were assembled, Jesus questioned them:

42 “What do you think about the Christ? Whose son is He?” “David’s,” they answered.

43 Jesus said to them, “How then does David in the Spirit call Him ‘Lord’? For he says:

44 ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at My right hand until I put Your enemies under Your feet.”’

45 So if David calls Him ‘Lord,’ how can He be David’s son?”

34 For David did not ascend into heaven, but he himself says: ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at My right hand

35 until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet.”’

36 Therefore let all Israel know with certainty that God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Christ!”

13 Yet to which of the angels did God ever say: “Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet”?

5 So also Christ did not take upon Himself the glory of becoming a high priest, but He was called by the One who said to Him: “You are My Son; today I have become Your Father.”

6 And in another passage God says: “You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.”

The original words

Tap a word to see its meaning.

Two readings, fairly stated

This is the Old Testament verse the New Testament quotes most often. Jesus uses it to ask how the Messiah can be David's son if David calls him 'my Lord.' The apostles use it for Jesus' ascension to God's right hand, and Hebrews draws on verse 4 for a king who is also a priest like Melchizedek.

Jewish interpreters commonly read the psalm as spoken about David, or about Abraham, by a court poet: 'The LORD said to my master.' In the Hebrew the second word is adoni, a title often used for human masters, which differs from the divine name and divine title.

Note. Jesus' question in Matthew 22 assumes his hearers already read the psalm as about the Messiah.
