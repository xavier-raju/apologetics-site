Resurrection and exaltation

The stone the builders rejected

typological

Old Testament · Psalm 118:22-23

22 The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.

23 This is from the LORD, and it is marvelous in our eyes.

New Testament

42 Jesus said to them, “Have you never read in the Scriptures: ‘The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. This is from the Lord, and it is marvelous in our eyes’?

10 then let this be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel: It is by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified but whom God raised from the dead, that this man stands before you healed.

11 This Jesus is ‘the stone you builders rejected, which has become the cornerstone.’

7 To you who believe, then, this stone is precious. But to those who do not believe, “The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone,”

The original words

Tap a word to see its meaning.

Two readings, fairly stated

Jesus applies this to himself after the parable of the tenants: the leaders reject him, but God makes him the cornerstone. Peter repeats it before the council after the resurrection.

Jewish readers commonly see the stone as Israel, despised by the nations but chosen by God, or as David, who was overlooked before he became king.

Note. Psalm 118 was part of the festival psalms (Hallel) sung at Passover; the crowds quoted 118:26 when Jesus entered Jerusalem.
