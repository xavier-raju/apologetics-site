Suffering and death

They will look on Me, the One they have pierced

direct predictionContested

Old Testament · Zechariah 12:10

10 Then I will pour out on the house of David and on the people of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and prayer, and they will look on Me, the One they have pierced. They will mourn for Him as one mourns for an only child, and grieve bitterly for Him as one grieves for a firstborn son.

New Testament

34 Instead, one of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water flowed out.

35 The one who saw it has testified to this, and his testimony is true. He knows that he is telling the truth, so that you also may believe.

36 Now these things happened so that the Scripture would be fulfilled: “Not one of His bones will be broken.”

37 And, as another Scripture says: “They will look on the One they have pierced.”

7 Behold, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him—even those who pierced Him. And all the tribes of the earth will mourn because of Him. So shall it be! Amen.

Two readings, fairly stated

God says the house of David will look on 'Me, the One they have pierced' and mourn as for an only son. John sees this in the soldier's spear piercing Jesus' side, and Revelation applies it to his return.

Jewish readers often take the pierced one as those killed defending Jerusalem in the battle of chapter 12, mourned by the nation. The Talmud (Sukkah 52a) connects the mourning with the slaying of a Messiah son of Joseph, so a messianic reading also exists in Jewish tradition.

Note. The Hebrew shifts from 'Me' to 'him' in the same verse, and some manuscripts read 'to him'. This affects how the verse is translated and is worth handling with care.
