The core case

The resurrection

Five facts almost every historian grants, one strong but contested fact, and every main theory weighed against them. Tap a card for the 30-second version, then go deeper.

Short on time?

The case in 30 seconds

Jesus died by crucifixion. Soon after, his followers, his sceptical brother James and a persecutor named Paul all said they had seen him alive, and they kept saying it under pressure. Nearly all scholars, sceptics included, grant those facts. A majority also think the tomb was found empty, though that one is debated. No natural theory explains all of it. You decide what does.

  • Gary Habermas, "The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus," Southeastern Theological Review 3/1 (2012)
  • William Lane Craig, "The Resurrection of Jesus," Reasonable Faith (popular writings)

The five Confirmed facts

These are the facts Gary Habermas calls minimal facts: each is granted by nearly all scholars who write on the subject, sceptics included. Only these five count in the theory tester.

01Confirmed

Jesus died by crucifixion

30-second version

Jesus was executed by crucifixion under Pontius Pilate. Christian and non-Christian sources agree, and almost no scholar doubts it.

Go deeper

Every Gospel ends with the cross, Paul's early creed begins with it (1 Corinthians 15:3), and the Roman historian Tacitus says Christus was executed under Pilate in the reign of Tiberius (Annals 15.44). Habermas lists the death by crucifixion as the first of his Minimal Facts, the short list that needs near-unanimous scholarly agreement. Michael Licona, working through the evidence at length, puts it in his 'historical bedrock' with two other facts, as Habermas reports. This fact matters for the tester because any theory that says Jesus did not really die has to overturn it.

What critics and critical scholars grant

  • They had believed Jesus was the Messiah, but then he got crucified

    Bart D. Ehrman, skeptic. William Lane Craig and Bart D. Ehrman, Is There Historical Evidence for the Resurrection of Jesus? The Craig-Ehrman Debate (transcript), 2006

  • Jesus' body decayed or was eaten by vultures, as was the case with most crucified persons.

    Gerd Lüdemann, skeptic. Gerd Lüdemann, A New Explanation of the Resurrection of Jesus: The Result of Mourning (Bible and Interpretation), 2012

  • the brute historical fact that Jesus would have died in agony on the cross

    John Dominic Crossan, skeptic. John Dominic Crossan (interview), PBS Frontline, From Jesus to Christ: Jesus' Many Faces, Arrest and Execution, 1998

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

The cross means God did not love us from a distance. Jesus entered real suffering and real death for people who had not earned it.

02Confirmed

The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

30-second version

Soon after his death, his followers had experiences they took to be meetings with Jesus alive. Even skeptical scholars grant they believed this.

Go deeper

The creed Paul passes on lists appearances to Cephas, the Twelve, more than five hundred at once, James and all the apostles (1 Corinthians 15:5-7). The Gospels and Acts tell of individual and group appearances. Habermas counts this as a core Minimal Fact, and Licona places it in the historical bedrock. The fact is about what the disciples experienced and believed, not yet about what caused it. That is why skeptics such as Gerd Lüdemann accept it and then offer hallucination as the cause (see the theory tester).

What critics and critical scholars grant

  • there were people who believed that they saw Jesus after He was dead after he had been crucified

    Dale C. Allison Jr., Christian (critical scholar). Dale C. Allison Jr. (interviewed by Pete Enns and Jared Byas), Episode 206: Dale C. Allison, Approaching the Resurrection of Jesus as a Historian (The Bible for Normal People, published transcript), 2022

  • Believers who knew he had been raised from the dead started having visions of him.

    Bart D. Ehrman, skeptic. William Lane Craig and Bart D. Ehrman, Is There Historical Evidence for the Resurrection of Jesus? The Craig-Ehrman Debate (transcript), 2006

  • Easter began with a visionary experience that happened to Peter.

    Gerd Lüdemann, skeptic. Gerd Lüdemann, A New Explanation of the Resurrection of Jesus: The Result of Mourning (Bible and Interpretation), 2012

  • Jesus' body decayed or was eaten by vultures, as was the case with most crucified persons.

    Gerd Lüdemann, skeptic. Gerd Lüdemann, A New Explanation of the Resurrection of Jesus: The Result of Mourning (Bible and Interpretation), 2012

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

The risen Jesus came back for friends who had run away. If he met them in their fear and doubt, he can meet you in yours.

03Confirmed

Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

30-second version

Saul of Tarsus tried to destroy the church, then became its best-known missionary, saying the risen Jesus appeared to him.

Go deeper

Paul writes in his own letters that he persecuted the church and then received a revelation of Jesus (Galatians 1:13-16), and he puts himself last in the list of appearances (1 Corinthians 15:8-9). Acts tells the story three times, starting in Acts 9. Habermas lists this among his Minimal Facts, and Licona's bedrock includes it. Paul is important for the tester because he was an enemy, not a grieving follower, so theories built on grief or wishful thinking struggle to explain him.

What critics and critical scholars grant

  • Some of them were actual visions like Paul

    Bart D. Ehrman, skeptic. William Lane Craig and Bart D. Ehrman, Is There Historical Evidence for the Resurrection of Jesus? The Craig-Ehrman Debate (transcript), 2006

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

No one is too far gone. Grace turned the church's fiercest opponent into one of its boldest messengers, and it can rewrite your story too.

04Confirmed

James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

30-second version

Jesus' brother James did not believe in him during his ministry, yet later led the church in Jerusalem. Paul says Jesus appeared to him.

Go deeper

John 7:5 says his brothers did not believe in him, and Mark 3:21 has his family trying to take charge of him. Paul says the risen Jesus appeared to James (1 Corinthians 15:7) and later met him as a leader in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:19). Josephus records his execution (Antiquities 20.200). Habermas says he goes back and forth on whether to count this as a Minimal Fact (2012, p. 25); Licona treats it as a strong 'second-order' fact because fewer scholars discuss it, not because many deny it (Habermas 2012, pp. 22-23). We label it Confirmed for the facts of the texts and note this nuance on the card.

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

If people in your family doubt, take heart. Even Jesus' own brother did not believe at first, yet he became a leader of the church.

05Confirmed

The resurrection was proclaimed very early

30-second version

The claim that Jesus was raised did not grow up decades later. Paul passes on a creed he had already received.

Go deeper

In 1 Corinthians 15:3 Paul says he delivered what he had received: that Christ died, was buried, was raised on the third day and appeared to named witnesses. Habermas says that for years he folded this early date into the appearances fact, then began to count it as a separate Minimal Fact, because few conclusions are better accepted across the scholarly spectrum (2012, pp. 24-25). Surveying the literature, Habermas reports that 'the vast majority of critical scholars who answer the question place Paul's reception of this material in the mid-30s A.D.' (2005, web copy). Paul likely received it on his visit to Peter and James (Galatians 1:18-19). Even the skeptic Gerd Lüdemann dates the appearance traditions to 'the time between 30 and 33 CE', as quoted by Habermas (Dialog 2006).

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

You stand on the same message the first believers stood on: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day.

The empty tomb

Strong, but not near-unanimous, so it gets its own card and is never counted as evidence in the tester.

Contested

The tomb was found empty

30-second version

The tomb was found empty on the Sunday. A fairly strong majority of scholars accept this, but it is not near-unanimous.

~75 / 25Habermas's count of arguments in the literature since 1975, not a poll

Go deeper

Mark 16:1-8 tells of women finding the tomb empty, and Matthew 28:11-15 says the authorities claimed the disciples stole the body, which assumes the body was missing. He is explicit that he has never counted it as a Minimal Fact, because it lacks near-unanimity (2012, p. 25). Craig, by contrast, lists it among his four facts. Habermas reports a count of arguments in the scholarly literature since 1975, not a formal poll: about 75% of the publications he counted favor one or more arguments for the empty tomb, and about 25% favor one or more against it.

What critics and critical scholars grant

  • the arguments in favor of an empty tomb are better than those against it

    Dale C. Allison Jr., Christian (critical scholar). Dale C. Allison Jr. (interviewed by Pete Enns and Jared Byas), Episode 206: Dale C. Allison, Approaching the Resurrection of Jesus as a Historian (The Bible for Normal People, published transcript), 2022

Why this matters Reflection, not evidence

Death does not get the last word, for Jesus or for anyone who trusts him. Every grave we stand beside is answered by the angel's words.

Also discussed, and contested

Shown with their label. None of these is used as evidence in the tester.

Contested

The resurrection was the centre of the earliest Christian message

30-second version

The first Christians did not treat the resurrection as a side doctrine. It was the heart of what they preached.

Go deeper

Paul calls the creed 'of first importance' (1 Corinthians 15:3) and says that if Christ is not raised, faith is useless (15:14, 17). Peter's first sermon in Acts 2 centres on it. Habermas has a few times counted this as a Minimal Fact, but he says himself the case is not quite as strong as for the early date (2012, p. 25), and Licona does not list it. We mark it Contested so the tester does not weigh it.

Contested

Jesus was buried in a tomb by Joseph of Arimathea

30-second version

All four Gospels say a member of the Jewish council, Joseph of Arimathea, put Jesus in a tomb. Craig counts this as his first fact.

Go deeper

Mark 15:42-46 and the other Gospels name Joseph of Arimathea as the man who buried Jesus, and the creed says simply that he was buried (1 Corinthians 15:4). Craig makes the burial the first of the four facts he argues 'any adequate historical hypothesis must account for' (Reasonable Faith article; also his opening statement against Ehrman in 2006). It is not on Habermas's Minimal Facts list, so we mark it Contested: it is argued for by many scholars but not near-unanimous.

Contested

Women were the first to find the tomb empty

30-second version

Every Gospel says women found the tomb empty. Habermas reports Licona treats female eyewitness testimony as an important issue.

Go deeper

Mark 16:1-8, Matthew 28:1-10, Luke 24:1-10 and John 20:1-2 all put women first at the tomb. Apologists argue that a story invented to persuade would not have chosen witnesses whose testimony carried less weight in that culture; Habermas notes Licona gives this question a full treatment (2012, p. 21, citing Licona pp. 349-55). The argument itself is our framing, and the fact depends on the empty tomb, which is Contested, so this record is Contested.

Contested

The disciples were changed and willing to suffer for their claim

30-second version

The first witnesses went from hiding to public preaching and accepted danger for it. Not every apostle's martyrdom is well attested.

Go deeper

Acts 2 shows Peter preaching publicly in Jerusalem weeks after the crucifixion. The site's honest note already says that only some apostles are well attested as martyrs (Peter, James son of Zebedee, James the brother of Jesus), and points to Sean McDowell's grading of each. The point is sincerity: people rarely suffer for something they know is false. It does not show their belief was true, and we mark it Contested at the level of individual apostles.

What critics and critical scholars grant

  • I think it is most likely by far that the original belief was derived from dreams, hallucinations, or "inspired" readings of scripture

    Richard Carrier, skeptic. Richard Carrier, Why I Don't Buy the Resurrection Story (6th ed., 2006), 2006
