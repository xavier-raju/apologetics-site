FramingOur argued judgment

The disciples stole the body

The disciples took the body and lied that he rose.

Explains 1 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  2. ✕

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Fails. Liars do not sincerely believe they saw him; the fact is about sincere belief.

    • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. A theft by the disciples gives an enemy no reason to convert.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. It does not explain why the skeptical brother believed.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ~

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Partly. A lie could be told early, but it leaves the sincere testimony unexplained.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

This is the oldest alternative: Matthew 28:11-15 says it was already being told. Habermas notes Hermann Reimarus revived it in the 18th century (CRJ 2001). It explains an empty tomb, but not why the disciples sincerely believed they had seen Jesus, nor why Paul the persecutor and James the skeptic came to believe. Our verdicts are argued judgments.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  • Berean Standard Bible, Matthew 28 (BSB), 2022 · Matthew 28

Jesus did not die (swoon)

Jesus fainted on the cross, revived in the tomb and was taken for risen.

Explains 0 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 2.

  1. ✕

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Fails. It denies the fact with the widest scholarly agreement.

    • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)
  2. ~

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Partly. Sightings of a survivor might happen, but not belief in a glorious risen Lord.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. It gives no account of Paul's experience years later.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. It gives no account of James's change.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ~

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Partly. Early claims are possible, but of a resuscitation, not a resurrection.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

Habermas notes Friedrich Schleiermacher favored this view and that David Strauss is usually thought to have crushed it, so that few scholars supported it after his critique (CRJ 2001). It runs straight into the best attested fact, the death by crucifixion. Even if a man survived, a half-dead figure would inspire pity, not belief that he had conquered death.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)

Individual hallucinations

Grieving followers had private hallucinations of Jesus.

Explains 2 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  2. ~

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Partly. It explains individual experiences, not the group appearances in the creed.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. Paul was a hostile persecutor, not a grieving disciple primed to see Jesus.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. James was a skeptic, not a grieving follower.

    • Berean Standard Bible, John 7 (BSB), 2022 · John 7
  5. ✓

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Explains. Hallucinations could be reported early.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

Strauss popularized this in the 19th century, and in recent decades Gerd Lüdemann and Jack Kent have argued versions of it (Habermas, CRJ 2001). It takes the appearance experiences seriously, which is its strength. But hallucinations leave the body in the tomb, and Paul was not a grieving follower. Habermas quotes psychologist Gary Collins that hallucinations are individual events that a group cannot share.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  • Paul Copan and Ronald K. Tacelli (eds.), Jesus' Resurrection: Fact or Figment? A Debate Between William Lane Craig and Gerd Lüdemann (IVP Academic), 2000 · publisher page

Group or mass hallucination

The groups who said they saw Jesus shared one hallucination or caught each other's excitement.

Explains 2 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  2. ~

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Partly. It tries to cover groups, but shared identical hallucinations are doubtful.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. Paul's experience came later and apart from the group.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. James was outside the circle of believers.

    • Berean Standard Bible, John 7 (BSB), 2022 · John 7
  5. ✓

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Explains. Group claims could be reported early.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

Lüdemann describes a 'chain reaction' spreading from Peter to the others, including the 500 (as summarized by Habermas, CRJ 2001). Habermas answers that most psychologists treat hallucinations as private; that the examples offered for collective hallucinations involve crowds who expected and wanted to see something; and that the disciples were in fear and grief, not expectation.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi

Paul's conversion disorder

Paul's vision came from guilt or psychological conflict, not from Jesus.

Explains 2 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  2. ✕

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Fails. On its own it covers only Paul; it needs another theory for the others.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  3. ~

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Partly. It offers a cause for Paul, though Paul gives no sign of guilt about persecuting before his vision.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. It says nothing about James.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ✓

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Explains. It does not conflict with an early report.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

Kent proposes that Paul experienced a conversion disorder, while the disciples had grief hallucinations; Habermas sets out and critiques this combination (CRJ 2001). Combining two theories to cover different witnesses multiplies the assumptions, and it still leaves the tomb untouched.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi

The story is a legend that grew over time

The resurrection story grew slowly from a simpler memory into a miracle tale.

Explains 1 of 5 Confirmed facts.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  2. ✕

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Fails. The fact is that the witnesses themselves believed it, not later storytellers.

    • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. Paul reports his own experience in his own letters.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. Paul met James in person (Galatians 1:19).

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  5. ✕

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Fails. An early creed leaves too little time for legend.

    • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)
Go deeper

Otto Pfleiderer and others thought legend could explain much of the data, though Habermas notes Pfleiderer himself admitted it could not explain it all (CRJ 2001). The main problem is time: the creed in 1 Corinthians 15 shows the claim of appearances to named people was already being passed on very early, before legend had time to grow.

  • Gary R. Habermas, Explaining Away Jesus' Resurrection: The Recent Revival of Hallucination Theories, Christian Research Journal 23/4, 2001 · web copy, endnotes i-xxxvi
  • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)

The women went to the wrong tomb

The women got lost, found an empty tomb that was not his, and the story spread.

Explains 1 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Mark 15 (BSB), 2022 · Mark 15
  2. ✕

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Fails. An empty tomb alone does not produce appearance experiences.

    • Gary R. Habermas, The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus: The Role of Methodology as a Crucial Component in Establishing Historicity, Southeastern Theological Review 3/1, 2012 · pp. 15-26 (pages cited per record)
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. It gives no account of Paul.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. It gives no account of James.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ~

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Partly. A mistaken report could spread early, but the creed centres on appearances.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

If the women went to the wrong tomb, the authorities or Joseph of Arimathea could have pointed to the right one. The theory is aimed only at the empty tomb, which is itself Contested, so in the tester it touches none of the Confirmed facts. We have not yet opened a primary source for its proponents, so none are named.

  • Berean Standard Bible, Mark 15 (BSB), 2022 · Mark 15
  • Berean Standard Bible, Mark 16 (BSB), 2022 · Mark 16

A twin or impostor was seen

Someone who looked like Jesus, perhaps a twin, was mistaken for him.

Explains 2 of 5 Confirmed facts, partly 1.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. It accepts that Jesus died.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Mark 15 (BSB), 2022 · Mark 15
  2. ~

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Partly. It could produce sightings, but needs close friends to be fooled repeatedly.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  3. ✕

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Fails. Paul calls it a revelation of the risen Jesus (Galatians 1:16), not a meeting with a look-alike.

    • Berean Standard Bible, Galatians 1 (BSB), 2022 · Galatians 1
  4. ✕

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Fails. A brother is the person least likely to be fooled by a look-alike.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ✓

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Explains. It does not conflict with an early report.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

No early source mentions a twin or look-alike. The theory needs an unknown person, an unknown motive, and a deception that fooled close friends, a hostile Pharisee and a skeptical brother. We have not opened a scholarly source naming a proponent, so none is named.

  • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
FramingOur argued judgment

God raised Jesus from the dead

Jesus really rose. This is the explanation the first witnesses gave.

Explains 5 of 5 Confirmed facts.

  1. ✓

    Jesus died by crucifixion

    Explains. On this view, he really died, then was raised.

    • William Lane Craig, The Resurrection of Jesus (Reasonable Faith, popular writings) · web page, section on the four facts and note 6
  2. ✓

    The disciples had experiences they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus

    Explains. On this view, they saw him because he was alive.

    • William Lane Craig, The Resurrection of Jesus (Reasonable Faith, popular writings) · web page, section on the four facts and note 6
  3. ✓

    Paul, a persecutor of the church, was turned around by what he believed was an appearance of Jesus

    Explains. On this view, the risen Jesus appeared to Paul, as Paul says.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  4. ✓

    James, the skeptical brother of Jesus, became a leader of the Jerusalem church

    Explains. On this view, the risen Jesus appeared to James, as Paul says.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
  5. ✓

    The resurrection was proclaimed very early

    Explains. On this view, the claim was early because the event was early.

    • Berean Standard Bible, 1 Corinthians 15 (BSB), 2022 · 1 Corinthians 15
Go deeper

Craig argues that this hypothesis passes the six tests historian C. Behan McCullagh lists for the best explanation: explanatory scope, explanatory power, plausibility, not ad hoc, in accord with accepted beliefs, and outstripping its rivals (Craig, Reasonable Faith article, citing McCullagh 1984 p. 19). Its cost is that it requires openness to God acting. That is a worldview question the reader has to weigh.

  • William Lane Craig, The Resurrection of Jesus (Reasonable Faith, popular writings) · web page, section on the four facts and note 6
