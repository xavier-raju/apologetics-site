The week before: from the last supper to the burial (20 entries)
  • FramingLife of Jesus 140

    Bread, wine, and covenant

    At his last meal, Jesus shares bread and wine and speaks of them as his body and his blood of the covenant.

    Mt 26:26-29 Lk 22:14-20 1 Cor 11:23-26

    Go deeper

    Matthew, Mark and Luke all place this at the final meal, and Paul gives a version in 1 Corinthians 11 that he says he received and passed on. The wording differs slightly between the four accounts. Paul's letter was written earlier than the Gospels, so it is the oldest written record of the scene.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 141

    Service, testing, and swords

    Luke reports that at the meal the disciples argue about who is greatest, and Jesus tells them to lead by serving.

    Lk 22:24-38

    Go deeper

    In Luke 22 Jesus also warns Peter that he will deny him, and he speaks about hard times ahead and about swords. Only Luke has this block of teaching at the meal. Luke's later arrest scene, where Jesus stops the violence, shows how the sword saying should be read.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 142

    John's farewell teaching

    According to John, Jesus gives his followers a new command at the meal: love one another as he has loved them.

    Jn 13:31-35

    Go deeper

    John 13 to 16 contains long farewell talks about his going to the Father, the coming Helper (the Spirit), the vine and branches, and the hatred his followers will face. These talks appear only in John. They read as John's own presentation of Jesus's last teaching, not as a word-for-word record.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 143

    The farewell prayer

    John reports a long prayer in which Jesus asks the Father to keep his followers safe and united.

    Jn 17

    Go deeper

    In John 17 Jesus speaks of finishing the work he was given and prays for his disciples and for those who will believe through them. No other Gospel has this prayer. Christians have drawn heavily on it for their teaching about unity.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 144

    Gethsemane or the Mount of Olives

    Matthew, Mark and Luke report that Jesus prays in deep distress on the Mount of Olives while his disciples fall asleep.

    Mt 26:36-46 Lk 22:39-46

    Go deeper

    Mark and Matthew name the place Gethsemane and have Jesus ask that the cup pass from him, yet accept the Father's will. Luke adds an angel and sweat like drops of blood, but some early manuscripts lack those two verses (Luke 22:43-44). John has no prayer scene, only the garden where the arrest happens.

    • Luke 22, note on 22:43-44 USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • FramingLife of Jesus 145

    Arrest and the severed ear

    All four Gospels report that Judas leads an armed group to arrest Jesus, and one disciple cuts off a servant's ear.

    Mt 26:47-56 Lk 22:47-53 Jn 18:1-12

    Go deeper

    Mark, Matthew and Luke have Judas identify Jesus with a kiss. John names the swordsman as Peter and the servant as Malchus, and Luke alone says Jesus healed the ear. In each account Jesus stops the fighting and the disciples scatter.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 146

    Questioning by Jewish authorities

    The Gospels report that Jesus is questioned by the high priest and Jewish leaders after his arrest.

    Mt 26:57-68 Lk 22:54, 63-71 Jn 18:13-24

    Go deeper

    Mark and Matthew describe a night hearing with false witnesses and a question about whether he is the Messiah, which ends in a charge of blasphemy. Luke places the council meeting in the morning. John instead has Jesus questioned by Annas and then sent to Caiaphas.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 147

    Peter denies association

    All four Gospels report that Peter denies knowing Jesus three times, just as Jesus had said he would.

    Mt 26:69-75 Lk 22:55-62 Jn 18:15-18, 25-27

    Go deeper

    The accounts agree on the three denials and on the courtyard setting at night, but differ on who challenges Peter. Mark has the rooster crow twice; the others mention one crow. Matthew, Mark and Luke say Peter weeps afterwards.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 148

    Judas's end

    Matthew reports that Judas returns the silver and hangs himself, while Acts gives a different account of his death.

    Mt 27:3-10

    Go deeper

    In Matthew 27 the priests use the returned money to buy a potter's field for burying strangers. Acts 1 says Judas himself bought a field and died there after a fall. Both connect his death to a field called the Field of Blood, but they tell it differently.

    • Matthew 27, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • FramingLife of Jesus 149

    Pilate and, in Luke, Herod

    All four Gospels report that the Jewish leaders hand Jesus over to Pilate, the Roman governor, who questions him.

    Mt 27:1-2, 11-14 Lk 23:1-5 Jn 18:28-38

    Go deeper

    Pilate asks whether Jesus is the King of the Jews. Luke alone adds that Pilate sends Jesus to Herod Antipas, who mocks him and sends him back. John gives the longest exchange, including Jesus's words about truth.

    • John 18, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • FramingLife of Jesus 150

    Barabbas and sentencing

    The Gospels report that Pilate offers to release Jesus or a prisoner named Barabbas, and the crowd chooses Barabbas.

    Mt 27:15-26 Lk 23:13-25 Jn 18:39-19

    Go deeper

    In the accounts Pilate is reluctant and finds no case against Jesus, but he gives in and hands him over to be crucified. Matthew adds Pilate washing his hands. A regular custom of releasing a prisoner at Passover has no clear support outside these accounts.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 151

    Flogging and royal mockery

    Mark, Matthew and John report that Roman soldiers flog Jesus and mock him as a king with a purple robe and a crown of thorns.

    Mt 27:26-31 Jn 19:1-3

    Go deeper

    The soldiers kneel in pretend homage and strike him. John places the flogging before the final sentence, while Mark and Matthew place it after. Luke does not narrate the mockery by the soldiers in the same way.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 152

    The route to execution

    Matthew, Mark and Luke report that a man named Simon of Cyrene is made to carry the cross for Jesus.

    Mt 27:32 Lk 23:26-32 Jn 19:17

    Go deeper

    Mark adds that Simon was the father of Alexander and Rufus, names his readers seem to know. Luke adds women mourning on the road. John says Jesus carried the cross himself and does not mention Simon.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 153

    Crucifixion at Golgotha

    All four Gospels report that Jesus is crucified at a place called Golgotha, between two other men, under a sign naming him King of the Jews.

    Mt 27:33-44 Lk 23:33-43 Jn 19:18-27

    Go deeper

    The accounts mention soldiers dividing his clothes and onlookers mocking him. Luke reports that one of the men crucified with him asks to be remembered and Jesus promises him paradise. John adds that Jesus entrusts his mother to the disciple he loved.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 154

    The sayings from the cross

    Each Gospel records different words of Jesus on the cross; the familiar list of seven comes from combining all four.

    Lk 23:34 Jn 19:26-27 Mt 27:46 Jn 19:28 Lk 23:46

    Go deeper

    Mark and Matthew give only the cry of abandonment from Psalm 22. Luke has words of forgiveness, the promise to the criminal, and a final prayer of trust. John has words to his mother, "I thirst", and "It is finished".

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 155

    Death and extraordinary signs

    The Gospels report that Jesus dies on the cross, and Matthew, Mark and Luke describe darkness at midday and the temple curtain torn.

    Mt 27:45-56 Lk 23:44-49

    Go deeper

    Mark and Matthew report a Roman centurion calling him God's Son, and Luke has the centurion call him innocent. Matthew alone adds an earthquake and holy people raised from tombs. All four note that women who followed him watched.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 156

    The pierced side and unbroken legs

    John reports that soldiers did not break Jesus's legs because he was already dead, but pierced his side, and blood and water came out.

    Jn 19:31-37

    Go deeper

    Only John tells this, and he stresses that it comes from an eyewitness. He links both details to Scripture: no bone broken, and looking on the one they pierced. The scene underlines that Jesus was really dead.

    • John 19, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
  • FramingLife of Jesus 157

    Joseph of Arimathea's burial

    All four Gospels report that Joseph of Arimathea asks Pilate for the body and lays it in a tomb.

    Mt 27:57-61 Lk 23:50-56 Jn 19:38-42

    Go deeper

    Mark calls Joseph a respected council member, and John adds that Nicodemus brought spices. The accounts say the tomb was cut in rock and closed with a stone, and Mark, Matthew and Luke note that women saw where he was laid. This burial story is the starting point for the later empty tomb reports.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • ContestedLife of Jesus 158

    Descent or proclamation to the dead: theological placement

    Later Christian creeds say Jesus went to the dead between his death and resurrection, based on a few disputed New Testament passages.

    1 Pet 3:18-20 Eph 4:8-10

    Go deeper

    1 Peter 3 speaks of Christ preaching to spirits in prison, and Ephesians 4 speaks of him descending to the lower regions. Interpreters disagree about what these verses mean. The New Testament gives no story of the event; fuller scenes appear only in later writings.

    • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
  • FramingLife of Jesus 159

    The tomb guard

    Matthew alone reports that the authorities seal the tomb and post a guard so the body cannot be stolen.

    Mt 27:62-66

    Go deeper

    The leaders ask Pilate for the guard because Jesus had said he would rise. Later in Matthew 28 the guards report what happened and are paid to say the disciples stole the body. Matthew says this story was still told in his day.

    • Matthew 27, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
    • Matthew 28, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition

    • FramingLife of Jesus 160

      Women find the tomb empty

      All four Gospels report that women, Mary Magdalene among them, find Jesus's tomb empty on the first day of the week.

      Mt 28:1-8 Lk 24:1-12 Jn 20:1-2

      Go deeper

      The accounts differ in which women go, what time it is, and whether they meet one or two angels or young men. In Mark's earliest ending the women run away and say nothing out of fear; in Matthew and Luke they report to the disciples. In John, Mary first thinks the body has been taken away.

      • Matthew 28, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
      • Mark 16, text and textual notes New American Bible Revised Edition, hosted by Bible Gateway
    • FramingLife of Jesus 161

      Peter and the beloved disciple inspect

      John reports that Peter and the beloved disciple run to the tomb and find the burial cloths but no body.

      Jn 20:3-10

      Go deeper

      John says the other disciple saw and believed, though they did not yet understand the Scripture. Luke 24:12 mentions Peter alone going to the tomb, a verse missing from a few manuscripts. John does not name the beloved disciple in this scene.

      • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
    • FramingLife of Jesus 162

      Appearances to Mary and the women

      John reports that the risen Jesus appears to Mary Magdalene, and Matthew reports that he meets the women as they leave the tomb.

      Jn 20:11-18 Mt 28:9-10

      Go deeper

      In John 20 Mary at first takes him for the gardener until he says her name. In Matthew 28 the women grasp his feet and worship him, and he sends them to tell the brothers. Paul's list of appearances in 1 Corinthians 15 does not mention the women.

      • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
    • FramingLife of Jesus 164

      An assembled group in Jerusalem

      Luke and John report that Jesus appears to his gathered disciples in Jerusalem, shows his wounds, and sends them out.

      Lk 24:36-49 Jn 20:19-23

      Go deeper

      In Luke he eats a piece of fish to show he is not a ghost and explains the Scriptures. In John he breathes on them and gives them the Holy Spirit, with Thomas absent. Whether the two Gospels describe the same occasion is a matter of interpretation.

      • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue
    • FramingLife of Jesus 165

      Thomas's encounter

      John reports that a week later Jesus appears again, and Thomas, who had doubted, calls him "My Lord and my God".

      Jn 20:24-29

      Go deeper

      Thomas had said he would not believe without touching the wounds. Jesus invites him to do so and blesses those who believe without seeing. Only John tells this story.

      • John 20 King James Version, hosted by Bible Gateway

    • FramingLife of Jesus 163

      Emmaus and the report about Simon

      Luke reports that two disciples walking to Emmaus meet the risen Jesus but recognize him only when he breaks bread.

      Lk 24:13-35

      Go deeper

      One of them is named Cleopas. On the road Jesus explains how the Scriptures speak of him. When they return to Jerusalem they hear that the Lord has appeared to Simon, an appearance Luke reports but does not narrate.

      • Luke 24, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
    • FramingLife of Jesus 168

      Five hundred, James, and other apostles

      Paul passes on an early list of people the risen Jesus appeared to, including Peter, the Twelve, over five hundred at once, James and Paul himself.

      1 Cor 15:3-8

      Go deeper

      In 1 Corinthians 15 Paul says he handed on what he had received: that Christ died, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared. He notes that most of the five hundred were still alive when he wrote. The list gives no places or stories and does not mention the women or the empty tomb.

      • 1 Corinthians 15, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
    • FramingLife of Jesus 169

      Ascension

      Luke and Acts report that Jesus leaves his disciples and is taken up into heaven.

      Go deeper

      Luke 24 places the departure near Bethany and could be read as happening on Easter day. Acts 1, by the same author, says he appeared over forty days and was taken up in a cloud from the Mount of Olives. Two angels then say he will return the same way.

      • Luke 24, text and notes USCCB, New American Bible Revised Edition
      • Acts 1 King James Version, hosted by Bible Gateway
    • FramingLife of Jesus 175

      Continuing heavenly work in Christian theology

      Several New Testament letters describe the risen Jesus as now speaking for believers before God and reigning until death is defeated.

      Go deeper

      Romans 8:34 and Hebrews 7:25 say he intercedes for his people. 1 Corinthians 15:20-28 calls him the firstfruits of those raised and says he reigns until every enemy, death included, is defeated. These are statements of Christian belief rather than reports of events.

      • 0xSero repo, life of Jesus catalogue

      Paul's encounter

      Paul writes that he saw the risen Jesus, and Acts tells the story of his encounter on the road to Damascus.

      1 Cor 9:1 Gal 1:11-17

      Go deeper

      In 1 Corinthians 9 and Galatians 1 Paul says in his own words that he saw the Lord and received his gospel by revelation. Acts 9 describes a light, a voice and three days of blindness. Paul had been persecuting the church before this.

      Acts reports that the risen Jesus speaks to Ananias about Paul and continues to guide Paul on his journeys.

      Acts 27

After Pentecost

  • FramingLife of Jesus 170

    Mark's later endings

    The longer ending of Mark (16:9-20) summarizes appearances and sends the disciples out, but it is missing from the oldest complete manuscripts.

    Go deeper

    Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus end Mark at 16:8, with the women afraid. A shorter ending also circulated. Scholarly editions print it apart from 16:1-8 and treat it as a later addition, so it is not a separate early witness.

    • Mark 16, text and textual notes New American Bible Revised Edition, hosted by Bible Gateway
  • FramingLife of Jesus 174

    Revelation's visions

    The book of Revelation opens with John's vision of the exalted Jesus standing among seven lampstands.

    Rev 1

    Go deeper

    John, exiled on Patmos, describes a figure with blazing eyes who says he was dead and is alive forever. The vision uses symbolic language from Daniel and the prophets. It is presented as a visionary revelation, not as a narrative of an appearance.
