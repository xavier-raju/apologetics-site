Doubting? Start here

You are welcome here

Doubt is not the opposite of faith. Thomas doubted, and Jesus did not send him away. He met him: "Put your finger here and look at My hands. Reach out your hand and put it into My side. Stop doubting and believe." (John 20:27, BSB)

A father once said to Jesus, "I do believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24, BSB), and Jesus helped him. You can bring honest questions too.

A simple way to begin

  1. Name your question. Write down the one thing that bothers you most. Then look for it under "Answer an objection" or type it into "Ask a question."
  2. Start with the center. The Christian claim stands or falls on one event, the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:3-8). Read the resurrection page first.
  3. Read a Gospel yourself. Mark is short and can be read in one sitting. John wrote his Gospel "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in His name." (John 20:31, BSB)
  4. Talk to someone. Questions are easier with a friend, a pastor or a church small group.
  5. Be honest with God. You can pray even while you are unsure. Jesus said, "Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28, BSB)

Talking with a friend about faith

Peter tells believers how to speak about their hope:

"But in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord. Always be prepared to give a defense to everyone who asks you the reason for the hope that is in you. But respond with gentleness and respect, keeping a clear conscience, so that those who slander you may be put to shame by your good behavior in Christ." (1 Peter 3:15-16, BSB)

Paul adds:

"Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how to answer everyone." (Colossians 4:6, BSB)

If you are the one sharing:

  • Listen first. Ask what they believe and why, and let them finish.
  • Ask questions. "What would change your mind?" often helps more than a long argument.
  • Be gentle and respectful. You are talking with a person, not winning a debate.
  • Be honest. If you don't know an answer, say so, and offer to look into it together.
  • Admit what is debated. Each answer card has a "Still debated" note. Use it.
  • Live it. Peter ties our words to "good behavior in Christ." People notice how we treat them.
  • Keep Jesus at the center. Side questions matter, but the main question is who Jesus is and whether He rose.
  • Leave room. You don't have to settle everything in one conversation. Pray for your friend and stay their friend.

If you are the one asking: it is fair to ask hard questions, and it is fair to ask Christians to show their work. That is what this site tries to do.

An invitation

When Nathanael doubted, Philip didn't argue. He invited him: "'Can anything good come from Nazareth?' Nathanael asked. 'Come and see,' said Philip." (John 1:46, BSB)

That is our invitation too.
