Life of Jesus timeline

From his birth to the earliest witnesses and manuscripts. Dates marked approx. are scholarly estimates.

  1. c. 6-4 BC (approx.) life Confirmed

    Birth of Jesus in Bethlehem

    Matthew and Luke place the birth under Herod the Great, who died in 4 BC by the usual reckoning, so most scholars date it about 6-4 BC. A minority dates Herod's death to 1 BC.

    Read:

    Places: Bethlehem

    Sources
    • Josephus, Antiquities 17 (Herod's death)
    • Matthew 2:1 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  2. 4 BC (conventional; minority view 1 BC) (approx.) history Confirmed

    Death of Herod the Great

    Herod's death sets the latest likely date for Jesus' birth.

    Read:

    Places: Herodium

    Sources
    • Josephus, Antiquities 17 (Herod's death)
    • Herod the Great overview

  3. c. 4 BC - AD 28 (approx.) life Confirmed

    Childhood in Nazareth

    Jesus grew up in Nazareth in Galilee, a small Jewish village. Archaeology shows the village was inhabited at the time.

    Read:

    Places: Nazareth

    Sources
    • K. Dark, "Early Roman-period Nazareth and the Sisters of Nazareth Convent," Antiquaries Journal 92 (2012)
    • Luke 2:39-52 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  4. AD 6 history Contested

    Census under Quirinius

    Josephus dates Quirinius's census of Judea to AD 6. How this fits Luke 2:2 and a birth under Herod is a long-debated question.

    Read:

    Sources
    • Josephus, Antiquities 18.1 (census of Quirinius)
    • Census of Quirinius overview

  5. AD 26-36 history Confirmed

    Pontius Pilate governs Judea

    Pilate served as Roman prefect of Judea. An inscription found at Caesarea names him with that title.

    Read:

    Places: Caesarea Maritima

    Sources
    • G. Alföldy, Scripta Classica Israelica 18 (1999) 85-108
    • Pilate stone overview

  6. c. AD 28-29 (approx.) ministry Confirmed

    Baptism by John and start of ministry

    Luke dates John the Baptist's work to Tiberius's 15th year (about AD 28/29) and says Jesus was about thirty. Historians count the baptism among the most secure facts about Jesus.

    Read:

    Places: Jordan River

    Sources
    • Luke 3:1-3 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  7. c. AD 28-30 (approx.) ministry Confirmed

    Ministry in Galilee

    Jesus taught and healed around the Sea of Galilee, with Capernaum as a home base, and called the Twelve.

    Read:

    Places: Capernaum (Tell Hum), Magdala, el-Araj (candidate for Bethsaida), Sea of Galilee

    Sources
    • Mark 1:14-21 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  8. c. AD 28-33 (approx.) ministry Confirmed

    Healing at the Pool of Bethesda

    John sets this healing at a Jerusalem pool near the Sheep Gate with five covered colonnades. Archaeology has found a pool complex that fits his description.

    Read:

    Places: Pool of Bethesda, Jerusalem

    Sources
    • John 5:1-9 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  9. c. AD 28-33 (approx.) ministry Confirmed

    Man born blind washes at Siloam

    Jesus sends a blind man to wash in the Pool of Siloam. A large Second Temple pool, widely identified as the Pool of Siloam, was found in 2004. Excavation of the rest of it is still under way.

    Read:

    Places: Pool of Siloam, Jerusalem

    Sources
    • John 9:1-7 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  10. about 1 to 3 years (approx.) ministry Confirmed

    Length of the ministry

    The first three Gospels fit a ministry of about a year, while John mentions three Passovers, pointing to two or three years.

    Read:

    Sources
    • John 2:13 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  11. AD 30 or 33 (approx.) passion Confirmed

    Last Supper

    Jesus shares a final meal with his disciples near Passover. Paul records the earliest written account of it.

    Read:

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Mark 14:12-25 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  12. AD 30 or AD 33 (both widely held) (approx.) passion Confirmed

    Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate

    Jesus was crucified on a Friday near Passover under Pilate. Scholars favor either AD 30 or AD 33. The first three Gospels and John differ on whether that day was Passover itself or the day of Preparation. This is one of the most widely accepted facts about Jesus.

    Read:

    Places: Golgotha (traditional site, Church of the Holy Sepulchre), Jerusalem

    Sources
    • C. J. Humphreys and W. G. Waddington, "Dating the Crucifixion," Nature 306 (1983) 743-746
    • Tacitus, Annals 15.44
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  13. AD 30 or 33 (approx.) passion Confirmed

    Burial in a tomb

    Joseph of Arimathea buries Jesus in a rock-cut tomb. The heel bone of Yehohanan shows that crucified men could receive a family burial.

    Read:

    Places: Golgotha (traditional site, Church of the Holy Sepulchre)

    Sources
    • Mark 15:42-47 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  14. AD 30 or 33, third day (approx.) resurrection Confirmed

    Empty tomb and resurrection proclaimed

    Women find the tomb empty on the first day of the week. The resurrection itself is a claim of faith; historians assess the empty tomb reports and the appearances.

    Read:

    Places: Golgotha (traditional site, Church of the Holy Sepulchre)

    Sources
    • Mark 16:1-8 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  15. AD 30 or 33, over about 40 days (approx.) resurrection Confirmed

    Resurrection appearances

    The Gospels, Acts and Paul report appearances to Peter, the Twelve, more than 500 at once, James and others. Most historians, skeptics included, accept that the disciples sincerely believed they had seen him alive.

    Read:

    Places: Jerusalem, Sea of Galilee

    Sources
    • Gary R. Habermas, "The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus," Southeastern Theological Review 3/1 (Summer 2012) 15-26
    • 1 Corinthians 15:5-8 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  16. AD 30 or 33 (approx.) church Confirmed

    Pentecost and first preaching in Jerusalem

    The apostles begin preaching publicly in Jerusalem that Jesus is risen.

    Read: Acts 2

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Acts 2 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  17. c. AD 31-36 (approx.) church Confirmed

    Conversion of Paul

    Paul, who had persecuted the church, says the risen Jesus appeared to him. Most date this within a few years of the crucifixion.

    Read:

    Places: Damascus

    Sources
    • Galatians 1:13 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • Conversion of Paul overview

  18. in the AD 30s (approximate) (approx.) church Confirmed

    Paul receives the resurrection creed

    In 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 Paul passes on an earlier creed he says he "received." Many scholars, skeptics included, think Paul received it in the 30s, likely when he met Peter and James in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:18-19).

    Read:

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Gary R. Habermas, "The Minimal Facts Approach to the Resurrection of Jesus," Southeastern Theological Review 3/1 (Summer 2012) 15-26, see p. 25
    • Galatians 1:18-19 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)

  19. c. AD 44 (approx.) church Confirmed

    James son of Zebedee killed

    Herod Agrippa I has the apostle James put to death.

    Read:

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Acts 12:1-2 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  20. c. AD 50-60 (approx.) writings Confirmed

    Paul's undisputed letters written

    Seven letters almost all scholars accept as Paul's, including 1 Corinthians, are the earliest Christian writings we have.

    Read:

    Places: Corinth, Rome

    Sources
    • R. E. Brown, An Introduction to the New Testament, Yale University Press (Anchor Yale Bible Reference Library), 1997
    • 1 Corinthians 15:1 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • See also: 0xSero dossier (link only)

  21. c. AD 53-55 (approx.) writings Confirmed

    1 Corinthians written

    Paul writes to Corinth and records the creed listing those who saw the risen Jesus, noting most of the 500 were still alive.

    Read:

    Places: Corinth

    Sources
    • 1 Corinthians 15:6 (Berean Standard Bible, primary text)
    • 1 Corinthians 15 overview

  22. AD 62 history Confirmed

    James the brother of Jesus executed

    Josephus records that the high priest Ananus had "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James" stoned. Scholars widely accept this passage as genuine.

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Josephus, Antiquities 20.200

  23. AD 64 history Confirmed

    Nero blames Christians for the fire of Rome

    Tacitus later describes Nero punishing Christians after the great fire.

    Places: Rome

    Sources
    • Tacitus, Annals 15.44

  24. c. AD 65-75 (some date earlier) (approx.) writings Contested

    Gospel of Mark written

    Most critical scholars date Mark to about 65-75; conservative scholars often date it earlier.

    Sources
    • R. E. Brown, An Introduction to the New Testament, Yale University Press (Anchor Yale Bible Reference Library), 1997
    • Gospel dating overview

  25. AD 70 history Confirmed

    Fall of Jerusalem and the Temple

    Roman forces under Titus capture Jerusalem and destroy the Temple, ending the Second Temple period.

    Read:

    Places: Jerusalem

    Sources
    • Josephus, The Wars of the Jews (Project Gutenberg)
    • Siege of Jerusalem (AD 70) overview

  26. c. AD 80-100 (some date earlier) (approx.) writings Contested

    Gospels of Matthew and Luke written

    Common critical ranges are Matthew about 80-95 and Luke about 80-100; many conservative scholars date both before AD 70.

    Sources
    • R. E. Brown, An Introduction to the New Testament, Yale University Press (Anchor Yale Bible Reference Library), 1997
    • Gospel dating overview

  27. c. AD 90-100 (some date it later, a few earlier) (approx.) writings Contested

    Gospel of John written

    Most scholars date John last of the four, around AD 90 to 100.

    Sources
    • R. E. Brown, An Introduction to the New Testament, Yale University Press (Anchor Yale Bible Reference Library), 1997
    • Gospel dating overview

  28. AD 93-94 witness Confirmed

    Josephus writes the Antiquities

    The Jewish historian Josephus mentions Jesus twice. The longer passage (18.63-64) most scholars see as a genuine core with later Christian additions; the brief mention of James (20.200) is widely accepted.

    Places: Rome

    Sources
    • Josephus, Antiquities 18
    • T. C. Schmidt, Josephus and Jesus, OUP 2025

  29. c. AD 100-200 (debated) (approx.) manuscript Contested

    Papyrus P52 (John 18) copied

    A small fragment of John 18, often called the oldest New Testament papyrus. Its first editor dated it to about 100-150; the Rylands Library notes recent research leans toward a date nearer 200. The honest range is second century.

    Read:

    Sources
    • John Rylands Library: P52

  30. c. AD 112 (approx.) witness Confirmed

    Pliny the Younger writes to Trajan about Christians

    As governor of Bithynia-Pontus, Pliny reports that Christians met on a stated day before light and "addressed a form of prayer to Christ, as to a divinity" (Letters 10.96, X.97 in the Melmoth/Bosanquet numbering).

    Places: Bithynia (region)

    Sources
    • Pliny, Letters 10.96 (X.97 in the Melmoth/Bosanquet numbering), our hosted Melmoth/Bosanquet text
    • Another translation (Georgetown, link only)

  31. c. AD 116 (about 115-117) (approx.) witness Confirmed

    Tacitus writes the Annals

    The Roman historian Tacitus says "Christus" was executed by Pontius Pilate in the reign of Tiberius. He uses the later title procurator rather than prefect.

    Places: Rome

    Sources
    • Tacitus, Annals 15.44

  32. c. AD 155-160 (approx.) witness Confirmed

    Justin Martyr's Dialogue with Trypho

    Justin records a long, respectful debate with a Jewish teacher over the prophecies about the Messiah.

    Places: Rome

    Sources
    • Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho (New Advent)

  33. late 2nd to early 3rd century (approx.) manuscript Confirmed

    Papyrus P137 (P.Oxy. 5345, Mark 1) copied

    A fragment of Mark 1, published in 2018. It was once rumored to be a first-century copy of Mark; the published dating is late second to early third century. It is not first-century.

    Read:

    Sources
    • Egypt Exploration Society: P.Oxy. LXXXIII 5345
    • Papyrus 137 overview

  34. c. AD 230-270 (approx.) archaeology Confirmed

    Frankfurt silver amulet buried

    A silver amulet with a Christian text naming Jesus is buried in a grave at Roman Frankfurt.

    Read:

    Places: Nida (Roman town at modern Frankfurt)

    Sources
    • Goethe University Frankfurt release (2024)
