What others say, and how to respond

Views from skeptics, other religions and groups outside historic Christianity, described as their own followers would, with a gracious response.

Our principles
  • Describe each view as its own followers would
  • Be respectful; never mock
  • Never cast Jewish people negatively
  • Start with common ground

Skeptics

Secular historians and skeptics

Most secular scholars accept that Jesus lived, taught, and was crucified, and that his followers believed he appeared to them. They doubt the miracles, often because history as a method cannot confirm a miracle.

Thank them for agreeing on so much. Then ask which explanation best fits the agreed facts, and whether ruling out God from the start is a conclusion or an assumption.

  • Jesus existed
  • He was crucified under Pilate
  • His followers sincerely believed
  • Answer card: The disciples hallucinated
Skeptics

Mythicists (Jesus never existed)

A small group argues Jesus was a myth or a copy of older gods.

Gently note that this view is rejected by nearly all historians, including non-Christian ones. Point to Paul meeting Jesus' brother, and to Tacitus and Josephus.

  • Asking for evidence is good
  • Answer card: Jesus never existed
Other religions

Judaism

Jewish tradition reads its Scriptures through centuries of careful interpretation. Many Jewish readers see the Messiah's work as still to come: world peace, the return of the exiles, and the rebuilding of the Temple. Since the Middle Ages most Jewish teachers have read the servant of Isaiah 53 as the people of Israel, a reading already known by the 3rd century.

Approach with deep respect and gratitude: Jesus and the apostles were Jewish, and the Scriptures came to us through the Jewish people. Christians must also own the painful history of Christian mistreatment of Jews. Then gently open Isaiah 53 together. Everyone in Isaiah confesses sin, the prophet too, but the servant alone is sinless. He suffers 'for the transgression of My people', restores Israel, dies, and then lives again, which points to one person, Jesus. You can also share that early Jewish sources such as the Targum and the Talmud connect the passage with the Messiah. Listen well, and never pressure.

  • One God
  • The Hebrew Scriptures
  • Hope in God's promises
  • Answer card: Isaiah 53 is about Israel
  • Prophecy explorer: The Suffering Servant
Other religions

Islam

Muslims honor Jesus (Isa) as a great prophet and the Messiah, born of a virgin, who worked miracles. The Qur'an (4:157) teaches he was not crucified, and Islam denies he is the Son of God.

Start with your shared love for Jesus. Then point to the crucifixion: every first- and early second-century source that mentions his death, Christian, Jewish, and Roman, affirms it, and these sources are five to six centuries earlier than the Qur'an.

  • Virgin birth
  • Jesus as Messiah
  • Jesus' miracles
  • One God
  • Answer card: No sources from his time
Other religions

Hindu, Buddhist, and spiritual-but-not-religious friends

Many see Jesus as a wise teacher or enlightened soul, one path among many.

Agree that Jesus' teaching is beautiful. Then note that he made claims no ordinary teacher makes (John 14:6) and that the resurrection is a historical claim worth checking.

  • Respect for Jesus' teaching
  • Spiritual hunger
  • The resurrection
Groups outside historic Christianity

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons)

Latter-day Saints love Jesus and call him Savior. They also accept the Book of Mormon and other scriptures, and they teach that God the Father has a glorified body and that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are three separate beings, not one God in three persons.

Be kind: they are often sincere, moral, and devoted. Talk about the nature of God (Isaiah 43:10; 44:6), grace (Ephesians 2:8-9), and whether the Bible needs additions. Note the lack of archaeological support for Book of Mormon peoples, gently.

  • Love for Jesus
  • Family
  • Bible reading
Groups outside historic Christianity

Jehovah's Witnesses

Jehovah's Witnesses teach that Jesus is God's first creation, also identified with Michael the archangel, and not God himself. They deny the Trinity and teach that Jesus rose as a spirit, not in his body.

Welcome them warmly. Read John 1:1-3 and John 20:28 (Thomas: 'My Lord and my God!', BSB) together. Point to Jesus' bodily resurrection in Luke 24:39 and John 2:19-21.

  • High view of the Bible
  • Evangelistic zeal
  • One God
  • The resurrection
Within historic Christianity

Catholic and Orthodox Christians

Catholic and Orthodox Christians hold the historic creeds, including the Trinity and the bodily resurrection, with the rest of historic Christianity. Some topics are Catholic-specific, such as papal authority and the books Catholics call deuterocanonical.

These are brothers and sisters in the creeds. Where differences arise, discuss them as family. Any Catholic-specific material on this site is labeled Catholic.

  • The Trinity
